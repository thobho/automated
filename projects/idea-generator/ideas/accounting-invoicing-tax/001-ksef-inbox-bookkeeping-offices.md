# 001 — KSeF inbox for bookkeeping offices, priced per client
**One-liner:** One screen where a biuro rachunkowe sees every client's incoming (purchase) KSeF invoices, whatever bookkeeping program the office uses, with accept/reject, a per-client archive and a year-end export; sold per active client on an annual plan. (Merges I010 and I024.)
**Field:** accounting-invoicing-tax | **Type:** SaaS
**Target customer:** Polish biura rachunkowe, owner or office manager, that run desktop or mixed accounting programs (not wFirma/Fakturownia, which already bundle a KSeF inbox). Count: sources disagree: "a little over 35,000 active offices serving over 2 million entrepreneurs" (podatki.biz, Dec 2022) and "over 40,000, three quarters serving fewer than 100 clients" (Comparic, undated); an Infor figure of about 70,000 looks like a wider count (all from a search summary, pages not opened, so treat as unverified). Estimate for the reachable niche: a few thousand offices.
**Problem:** Since KSeF became mandatory (1 Feb 2026 large firms, 1 Apr 2026 everyone else, 1 Jan 2027 the smallest) an office has to pull purchase invoices of dozens of clients from the ministry's system, under per-client permissions and certificates, and chase clients about rejected or missing documents. Offices' extra KSeF costs "tied to the number of client invoices pulled" are described as potentially several thousand PLN per month (no per-invoice rates given).
**MVP scope (≤6 weeks):** (1) Office onboarding: upload one certificate for the office NIP, import the client list (CSV/NIP), a checklist showing which clients granted which KSeF permission. (2) Nightly incremental download of purchase invoices per client via the KSeF 2.0 API. (3) Inbox UI: filter by client, status (new, accepted, rejected, not in my books), PDF/XML preview, internal notes. (4) Export of accepted documents as CSV/XML/JPK-style files for common programs (start with 1–2 targets chosen after interviews). (5) Per-client archive with year-end ZIP of XML+PDF. Not in MVP: issuing invoices, OCR, bookkeeping.
**Why now:** KSeF is mandatory in three waves, the last on 1 Jan 2027, with KSeF numbers required in transfers from that date (official page and KPMG alert, see Sources). wFirma's help says KSeF tokens only work until the end of 2026 and are "not recommended" for mass authorization, so offices move to certificates (which a neutral tool must handle).
**Foreign analogues:** Dext (UK/global), practice plans billed per client, 10-client minimum, 20 clients at about £276–299 per month (Datamolino 2026 comparison, a competing vendor); AutoEntry (UK) bills per document credit; Hubdoc is free with Xero. They show accountants pay per client for "documents arrive pre-sorted" workflows. No public revenue figures found for them (unverified).
**Competition (PL / global):** Fakturownia: free accountant panel "with KSeF integration" that imports cost invoices for all clients, clients accept or reject, export to Symfonia/RAKS; or discounted client accounts from 4.99 PLN net per account per month (100-account Start package, 5,988 PLN/year). wFirma: office plan from 18 PLN net per client per month including accounting, HR and KSeF "without limits" (sponsor article, promotional); mass authorization and overnight download of purchase invoices into drafts, but only on wFirma packages that include accounting. Comarch Optima: documents pulled by client or office, passed on through Internetowa Wymiana Dokumentów. Symfonia and Saldeo also bundle KSeF (Saldeo pricing not found). Gap: offices on programs without a built-in inbox, and offices wanting one neutral view across programs. Counter-finding: KSeF connectivity is priced at about zero for small firms (L3-F26), so the standalone price must be small.
**Evidence of pain:** GOFIN KSeF forum: 22 new threads in 11 weeks, e.g. "Jak wystawić fakturę przez Aplikację Podatnika w imieniu klienta" and "Faktura z numerem KSeF, której nie mam w KSeF" (L6-F02), but these are how-to questions, not tooling complaints. The official KSeF app has 2.03/5 from 645 iOS ratings and 12 of 51 sampled reviews ask for notifications of new purchase invoices (L6-F01), which is end-user pain, not office pain. No direct complaint from an office about the lack of a multi-client inbox found. Facebook groups of accountants not readable. Search-trend data not checked (Google Trends not accessible to this agent): unverified.
**Monetization:** Office pays per active client per month, billed annually. Price point estimate 6–12 PLN net per client per month (below wFirma's 18 PLN bundle, above Fakturownia's 4.99 PLN client-account price). Offices bill clients 150–500 PLN per month for a JDG (cenauslug.pl comparison, commercially motivated), so a 1–2% pass-through is plausible but unproven.
**Revenue sketch (estimate):** Assumptions: 8 PLN per client per month, average 40 clients per signing office. 6 months: 15 offices x 40 = 600 clients = 4,800 PLN MRR. 12 months: 40 offices x 40 = 1,600 clients = 12,800 PLN MRR. That needs about 40 offices out of a few thousand in the niche (about 1% reach). Optimistic given free incumbents; a more realistic case is 15–25 offices, 5,000–8,000 PLN MRR.
**First 10 customers:** Find offices via the KIBR/Krajowa Izba Biur Rachunkowych and local chambers, accountants' Facebook groups and the GOFIN forum (answer KSeF questions, then offer a free pilot). Cold outreach to offices whose websites name Optima, Rewizor, Symfonia or Rachmistrz (public websites, listable). 10 pilots at 0 PLN for 60 days, then annual plan.
**Build complexity:** medium. Risks: KSeF 2.0 API session/rate-limit handling and incremental retrieval (integrator guide dated 05.05.2026, official Java and C# clients, no Python/Node client), holding clients' certificates (security duty, encryption at rest, audit logging), per-client permission expiry (wFirma notes yearly renewal), export formats for third-party programs (undocumented targets, may need manual testing). No ministry certification needed for integrators (L7-F02).
**Pros / Cons / Risks:** Pros: public API, no approval, B2B annual billing, data accumulates (a year of invoices raises switching cost; wFirma's own help says invoices do not transfer via CSV, L9-F15). Cons: the two biggest cloud invoicing tools give offices the same function free or at 18 PLN; offices on desktop programs may wait for their vendor's KSeF module; per-client price is tiny, so 10,000 PLN MRR needs 1,000+ clients. Risks: ministry features (notifications, event model) could erase the gap; secret-handling liability; accountants' conservative buying cycle.
**Scores (1–5):** L1 2, L2 2, L3 3, L4 2, L5 4, L6 3, L7 4, L8 3, L9 3 | **Weighted:** 2.9 / 5 | **Chance band:** 5–15% (just above the `<5%` cut at 2.8; competition evidence lowered L2 from 3 to 2, L3 from 4 to 3 and L9 from 4 to 3 versus the shortlist; lower end of the band)
**Cheapest validation test (≤1 week, ≤200 PLN):** Landing page ("Jedna skrzynka KSeF dla wszystkich klientów biura, 8 PLN/klient/mies.") plus a 10-question interview script; reach 30 offices through public KSeF forum threads and direct emails to offices that name Optima/Symfonia/Rewizor; success is 5 interviews and 3 offices agreeing to a paid pilot at a stated price. Spend: ~100 PLN on a domain and email tool.
**Kill criteria:** Fewer than 3 of 15 interviewed offices on non-bundling programs say the missing inbox is a problem they would pay for; or two or more say their vendor ships a multi-client KSeF inbox before launch; or the first 5 offices want a price at or below 4 PLN per client.
**Confidence:** low
