# 007 — Delegated KSeF access and token/certificate manager for bookkeeping offices
**One-liner:** A small dashboard for a biuro rachunkowe that shows, per client, which KSeF permissions the office and each employee actually hold, which certificates or tokens are about to expire, and which clients have not granted access yet, with reminders and a one-click checklist for the client. Overlaps strongly with idea 001 (same payer), and is probably best sold as a module of it.
**Field:** accounting-invoicing-tax | **Type:** SaaS
**Target customer:** Owner or office manager of a biuro rachunkowe with 20+ clients that must onboard clients into KSeF and keep access alive. Count: sources disagree, "a little over 35,000 active offices serving over 2 million entrepreneurs" (podatki.biz, Dec 2022) and "over 40,000, three quarters with fewer than 100 clients" (Comparic, undated); both via search summaries, unverified.
**Problem:** Access to KSeF is permission-based: the client grants an office `InvoiceRead`/`InvoiceWrite` (with `canDelegate`), the office grants staff access per client or for all clients; grants stay active until revoked and any time limit must be implemented by the client system (ksef-docs, page dated 10.07.2025). KSeF certificates are one-use identities (a revoked certificate cannot be reused; the number of certificates per identity is capped) (ksef-docs, 03.02.2026). Rights given on the paper ZAW-FA form must also be revoked with a ZAW-FA. Rights granted in KSeF 1.0 had to be reconfigured for KSeF 2.0 (search-result summary). Tokens are expected to be phased out for certificates in 2027, but a repository collaborator closed the "keep tokens" issue on 2026-09-23 with "tokeny zostają ale na nowych zasadach" (not a formal ministry statement).
**MVP scope (≤6 weeks):** (1) Office login, client list import (NIP list), per-client status board. (2) Read-only API sync of permissions via the `permissions/query` endpoints under the office's own certificate: who holds what for each client. (3) Checks: missing grant, grant without `canDelegate`, staff member without access to a client, certificate near end of use or revoked (rules to verify against the API). (4) Email reminders to the office and a client-facing checklist page ("nadaj uprawnienie w Aplikacji Podatnika" with screenshots). (5) CSV export for audits. Not in MVP: invoice download, issuing, bookkeeping.
**Why now:** KSeF is mandatory in waves up to 1 Jan 2027, and the token-versus-certificate rules are still moving (official stage page, GitHub issue above). Every office onboarding clients in the 2026/27 waves hits permission setup.
**Foreign analogues:** None found with traction (searched only Polish context; other countries' e-invoicing portals not checked, unverified). Adjacent model: per-client onboarding/status tools for tax practices (not researched here).
**Competition (PL / global):** Incumbents embed this in their products: wFirma mass authorization with a client-list status column, green/grey KSeF connection, notes on renewal (wFirma help); Fakturownia's free accountant panel shows invoice and KSeF status per client and manages client and staff permissions; Comarch Optima, Symfonia describe office workflows; an advertorial describes a "Moduł Certyfikatów i Uprawnień" in another invoicing program (sponsored, no pricing). The KSeF taxpayer application itself lists permissions. A standalone cross-program view does not appear in my results, but I did not search exhaustively.
**Evidence of pain:** GitHub CIRFMF/ksef-api issue #565 on tokens vs certificates: opened 2026-01-16, about 73 comments by the shortlist's earlier count (the fetched page showed 28 plus 45 collapsed), with "biura księgowe wymagają od przedsiębiorców tego certyfikatu tylko po to, żeby móc w jego imieniu wysyłać i odbierać faktury"; issue #794 (events/webhooks, 103 comments) and #786 (duplicates, 72) (L6-F06). These are from developers, not offices. Polish autocomplete: "ksef biuro rachunkowe…" suggests "uprawnienia", "jakie uprawnienia", "certyfikat" (L6-F23, autocomplete is not volume). GOFIN forum: threads on issuing on behalf of clients (L6-F02). No complaint of an office about missing a permissions dashboard found. Search trend: unverified.
**Monetization:** 3–6 PLN net per client per month or 99–249 PLN per office per month (estimate; compare Fakturownia's 4.99 PLN client accounts and wFirma's bundle at 18 PLN per client, where permissions are included). Payer: the office.
**Revenue sketch (estimate):** Assumptions: 149 PLN per office per month. 6 months: 8 offices = about 1,200 PLN MRR. 12 months: 25 offices = about 3,700 PLN MRR. 10,000 PLN MRR needs about 67 offices, plausible in count but only if incumbents' built-in features are not enough, which is not shown.
**First 10 customers:** The same channels as 001 (GOFIN forum, accountants' groups, KIBR), plus a free "KSeF permission audit" for 10 offices as a lead magnet. Merge outreach with 001 interviews.
**Build complexity:** low–medium. Risks: needs each office's certificate (custody and security duty), asynchronous grant operations, rules still changing, few permission events to test beyond the official TEST/DEMO environments.
**Pros / Cons / Risks:** Pros: very small build, annual-ish need, data it collects feeds 001. Cons: incumbents bundle it, ministry can add UI, the pain is intermittent (onboarding wave and renewals), weak standalone price. Risks: temporary need after the 2026/27 onboarding peak, certificate secrets, rule changes.
**Scores (1–5):** L1 1, L2 2, L3 2, L4 1, L5 4, L6 3, L7 4, L8 3, L9 3 | **Weighted:** 2.6 / 5 | **Chance band:** <5% (shortlist 3.0; L2 and L3 lowered after finding built-in office features, L4 1 for no revenue proof, L6 3 because evidence is developer-side; recommend folding into 001)
**Cheapest validation test (≤1 week, ≤200 PLN):** Do not build. Add three questions to the 001 interviews: how many clients are still not connected, how much time permission setup takes per client, and whether they would pay 4 PLN per client for a status board. Success: 5 of 10 offices report more than 15 minutes per client and name an unsolved failure case. Cost: nil.
**Kill criteria:** Fewer than 3 of 10 offices report ongoing permission problems; or the ministry or the top two programs ship a cross-client permission overview; or interviews show offices only need the feature during onboarding (first half of 2027).
**Confidence:** low
**Devil's advocate:** A permission dashboard is a screen, not a business. Every tool that pulls client invoices must already query the same permissions to work, so it shows "connected / not connected" per client as a by-product: wFirma's status column and Fakturownia's accountant panel do this today, FakturaXL publishes a permissions guide for its own users ([fakturaxl.pl](https://www.fakturaxl.pl/jak-zarzadzac-uprawnieniami-z-ksef)), and the state's own Moduł Certyfikatów i Uprawnień (MCU) has been the central place to manage access since 1 Nov 2025 (Fillup guide via search summary, unverified). A second search found no standalone cross-client permission tool, but also no office asking for one; the strongest advice found is to keep a simple register of who has which access (a spreadsheet). The need peaks during onboarding (2026 waves, 1 Jan 2027 for the smallest, token-to-certificate switch) and then becomes occasional, which fits a one-off audit service or a feature, not a subscription. At 149 PLN per office it needs 67 offices that are not already on a bundling program, the same narrow niche as 001 (which this review lowered to <5%). Selling it separately would split the founder's 10 h/week across two products for one buyer.
