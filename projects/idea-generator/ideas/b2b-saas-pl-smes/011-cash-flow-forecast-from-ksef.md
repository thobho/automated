# 011 — Cash-flow forecast built from KSeF invoices ("Prognoza płynności")
**One-liner:** A weekly 13-week cash forecast for Polish micro and small firms, built automatically from the issued and received invoices in KSeF plus fixed obligations (ZUS, PIT/VAT dates, leases), with an alert when the balance is projected to go negative.
**Field:** b2b-saas-pl-smes | **Type:** SaaS
**Target customer:** Owner-managed Polish firms with 5–50 employees that invoice B2B on credit terms, and their bookkeeping offices as resellers. Count: about 1.75 million active VAT payers are in KSeF scope (L5-F03); the share that would pay for a forecast is unverified.
**Problem:** Late customer payments hit liquidity. BIG InfoMonitor (as reported by podatki.biz) puts about 48% of Polish firms as struggling to collect receivables on time, micro-firms most; an EGB Finanse study via bank.pl says 69% of micro-entrepreneurs report liquidity problems from long trade credit (secondary reports, methodology not read). Whether owners want a forecasting tool, rather than a bank overdraft or a late-payment chase, is not shown.
**MVP scope (≤6 weeks):** (1) KSeF token/certificate login and nightly import of sales and purchase invoices (the ministry's API is public with no certification, L7-F02); (2) due-date ledger with expected payment date per customer learned from history; (3) bank balance via CSV upload (no PSD2 licence: avoid account-information provider status; unverified whether an AISP intermediary is needed); (4) 13-week chart, "balance below X" e-mail alert; (5) Stripe subscription. Not in MVP: bank feeds, scenarios, multi-entity.
**Why now:** KSeF puts every B2B invoice into one machine-readable source: mandatory for large taxpayers from 1 Feb 2026, everyone else from 1 Apr 2026, the smallest from 1 Jan 2027 (official stage page; L5-F01). That removes the manual entry that made forecasting tools a chore. Nothing forces the purchase, so this is an enabler, not a trigger.
**Foreign analogues:** Float (UK/IE): cash-flow forecasting from Xero and QuickBooks; Growth £159/month billed annually (£199 monthly), Small Business "from £99/mo", claims "8k+ finance teams" (own page). Pulse: entry plan listed at $29/month on G2 (search summary; not opened). Fathom: from A$59/month per company (per Float's blog; competitor source). The pattern is proven where the accounting system exposes an API; Poland has had no such data source until KSeF.
**Competition (PL / global):** Searches for a Polish forecasting product that imports KSeF found none (2026-10-29), but search is weak evidence. Invoicing apps already show finance statistics: inFakt Faktury 4.99 PLN/month net (annual billing) with KSeF and bank link and "statystyki finansowe" (search summary of inFakt's price page); Fakturownia and wFirma are invoicing and accounting. A PARP-listed project "PaymentsX" by Columb Technologies SA is described as AI cash-flow forecasting for SMEs; commercial availability unverified. Global tools (Float, Pulse, Fathom) are English-only and tied to Xero/QuickBooks. Gap: Polish-language forecasting on KSeF data; unproven demand.
**Evidence of pain:** Liquidity pain is documented; demand for a forecast is not. Quotes from reports: "około 48% firm ma kłopoty z terminowym odzyskiwaniem należności" (BIG InfoMonitor via podatki.biz); Intrum's European Payment Report 2026 (via trans.info): the gap between due date and actual payment in B2B grew from 16 days (2023) to 20 days (2026). No Wykop/Reddit/Facebook requests for a "prognoza cash flow" app were found. Fakturownia publishes cash-flow explainers (vendor content marketing), which suggests the topic draws readers but not that they pay. Search-trend data: unreadable, unverified.
**Monetization:** Subscription 49–99 PLN/month per firm, or 199 PLN/month for an office with 10 clients (estimates). Payer: owner or accountant. Anchor: Float £99–£199/month in the UK; Polish invoicing apps cost 5–45 PLN/month (search summary), so 49+ PLN needs clear value.
**Revenue sketch (estimate):** Assumes 69 PLN average. 6 months: 30 paying = 2,100 PLN MRR. 12 months: 100 = 6,900 PLN MRR. 10,000 PLN MRR needs about 145 customers, which is about 0.008% of 1.75 million payers, so reachability is a distribution issue, not a market size one.
**First 10 customers:** Bookkeeping offices that already sell "doradztwo" to growing clients: ask 20 offices (from the same lists as idea 001) to run the forecast for 3 clients each; Polish founder groups; a free "13-week cash sheet" lead magnet.
**Build complexity:** medium. Risks: KSeF token/certificate custody and token sunset, predicting payment dates from sparse history, bank balance input without PSD2 access, forecast accuracy for micro-firms with irregular cash.
**Pros / Cons / Risks:** Pros: data now available by API; B2B recurring; the dashboard is easy to test. Cons: no one was heard asking, price anchor in Poland is very low, owners may rely on the bank app, accuracy disappointments cause churn. Risks: platform dependency on KSeF API changes, security liability for credentials, regulatory edge if bank data is connected.
**Scores (1–5):** L1 2, L2 3, L3 2, L4 2, L5 3, L6 2, L7 4, L8 2, L9 2 | **Weighted:** 2.4 / 5 (computed 2.44) | **Chance band:** <5% (T16 review: L3 3→2 and L9 3→2, see Review changes; write-up value was 2.7, <5%; shortlist 2.9)
**Cheapest validation test (≤1 week, ≤200 PLN):** Build a spreadsheet forecast from a volunteer's KSeF export, show it to 10 owners or 5 offices, ask for a 49 PLN/month pre-order. Success: 3 of 10 owners ask to keep receiving it weekly and 1 pays.
**Kill criteria:** Fewer than 3 of 15 interviewed owners say they would pay 49 PLN/month; or offices say the forecast is already in their accounting package.
**Confidence:** low
**Devil's advocate:** The only party that holds both halves of the data this product needs, the bank balance and the KSeF invoices, is the firm's bank, and it already gives the invoice half away. mBank's mOrganizer finansów runs inside the business account, pulls purchase invoices from KSeF and sends sales invoices automatically after a one-time certificate setup; the basic invoicing module is "darmowy i nielimitowany", and the Pro tier lists cost invoices paid and due "z przypomnieniami o terminach zapłaty" (mBank pages, 2026-10-29). A 13-week forecast is a small step for a bank that already sees balances, standing orders, ZUS/tax transfers and now invoices, while this MVP must ask owners to upload bank CSVs by hand (it avoids PSD2 account-information status), which is the manual chore the "why now" claimed KSeF removes. A second search in Polish ("prognoza cash flow KSeF", bank and accounting-vendor variants) again found no Polish forecasting product, but also no owner or office asking for one: the pain evidence is about late payment, whose remedy is chasing or factoring, not a chart. Nobody in Poland is shown paying for forecasting at any price; the nearest anchors are inFakt at 4.99 PLN/month and free bank invoicing, against a planned 49–99 PLN. Float's £99–£199/month buyers are finance teams on Xero/QuickBooks, not 5–50-person owner-managed firms. Forecast accuracy for micro-firms with irregular cash is poor, so disappointment churn is likely.
**Review changes:** L3 3→2 (no Polish payer found for forecasting at any price; local anchors are 0–5 PLN invoicing bundles, so the 49–99 PLN price is not backed by anything similar customers pay today), L9 3→2 (banks hold balance plus KSeF data in a free in-account tool and can add a forecast at will; manual bank CSV input and accuracy misses push churn up), weighted 2.7→2.4, band stays <5%. Not rejected: a second search still found no Polish KSeF-based forecasting product, and the spreadsheet test in the write-up costs almost nothing; it is a weak <5% candidate whose main risk is that banks ship the feature.
