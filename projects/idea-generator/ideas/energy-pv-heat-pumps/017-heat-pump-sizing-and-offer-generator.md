# 017 — Heat-pump sizing and offer generator for Polish installers
**One-liner:** A browser tool where a small installer enters a house (area, insulation, windows, current fuel use), gets a room-by-room heat-load estimate and a recommended heat-pump size, and exports a branded Polish offer (oferta) with the Czyste Powietrze eligibility notes and a ZUM-list model.
**Field:** energy-pv-heat-pumps | **Type:** SaaS
**Target customer:** Small installation firms (1–10 staff) selling air-to-water heat pumps and PV to homeowners in Poland. Count: no installer count found (search 2026-10-29); unverified. Market size: PORT PC estimates about 80,000 air-to-water pumps sold to end customers in 2025 (including about 19,000 from stock) and about 6,600 ground-source (globenergia.pl, via search summary).
**Problem:** Sizing needs a heat-demand calculation per PN-EN 12831 before choosing a pump; a Panasonic expert at a Globenergia webinar warns of using catalogue values, a single method, oversizing (cycling, shorter life) and undersizing (more backup use), plus electrical mistakes (pumps above 9 kW usually need three-phase power; a 25 A protection on a pump needing 32 A caused trips). Installers bear responsibility for sizing errors, per the same article's reading (legal accuracy not assessed).
**MVP scope (≤6 weeks):** (1) Wizard for building data and a simplified room-by-room heat-load estimate (clearly labelled as an estimate, not a PN-EN 12831 certificate); (2) heat-pump catalogue (5–8 brands, sizes, COP at design temperature) with a "recommended / oversized / undersized" check and electrical requirement flags; (3) offer PDF with installer branding, price lines and options; (4) Czyste Powietrze eligibility notes and ZUM-list reminder; (5) subscription. Not in MVP: hydraulic design, certified energy audit, CRM, financing.
**Why now:** Czyste Powietrze: from 2027 and after a transition the programme funds only heat pumps among electric heating; advance-payment deadline extended to 180 days and rates +10% for some items from 20 Jul 2026; about 70,000 applications and 53,000 agreements worth 3.3 bn PLN since 31 Mar 2025 (L5-F22, Globenergia). Caveat: sales were flat in 2025, with the subsidy share falling (PORT PC via search), so the trigger is not a clear growth driver.
**Foreign analogues:** Aireo by Correla (UK): single plan £35/month, 30-day free trial, room-by-room heat loss, radiator schedules, heat-pump sizing, MCS 020/031 documents, "hundreds of users" (own page). Heat Engineer Software (UK): Capterra lists from £40 flat rate, four heat-loss methods, MCS support (listing). OpenSolar (Australia): free solar design and proposals, 28,000+ active professionals (own claim), no heat-pump features on its home page and no Polish interface (L1-F15). Revenue for any: none found.
**Competition (PL / global):** Manufacturer tools: Panasonic Aquarea Designer (online, login via Panasonic Pro Club; sizing, hydraulic scheme, energy label), WP-OPT simulation program (SPF, annual costs; price not found). Polish PV tools exist (EasySolar, SOLCAD, BlueSol, PV BAKS, L1-F15) but their heat-pump features and prices are unverified. Foreign: Aireo, Heat Engineer. Germany: pro software costs "over 6000 EUR including training" in one forum post and Heizlast as a service 480–720 EUR (older, undated forum figures). Gap: an independent, brand-neutral Polish offer generator at about 150 PLN/month; unproven demand.
**Evidence of pain:** Expert commentary rather than customer complaints: Globenergia webinar write-up (sizing errors, electrical mismatches, installer liability). German consumer-advice material (Verbraucherzentrale via reduco.ai, a vendor guide) says offers without a room-by-room heat-load calculation and documented hydraulic balancing do not get BAFA/KfW funding: the German analogue is a funding-driven calculation duty (not verified against the funding terms). No Polish installer thread asking for such a tool was read. Search-trend data: unreadable, unverified.
**Monetization:** 99–199 PLN/month per firm (estimate), one-off 99 PLN per offer pack as an alternative. Anchor: Aireo £35 (about 170 PLN, converted at an assumed 4.9 PLN per GBP). Payer: installer firm.
**Revenue sketch (estimate):** Assumes 149 PLN average. 6 months: 15 firms = 2,200 PLN MRR. 12 months: 45 firms = 6,700 PLN MRR. 10,000 PLN MRR needs about 67 firms; the market size is unverified, and seasonal sales (autumn peak) may cause churn.
**First 10 customers:** Small installers listed from Google Maps, from subsidy-installer lists and PV/heat-pump Facebook groups; offer a free offer template; partner with a distributor that supplies installers (one named wholesaler per region, to be found).
**Build complexity:** medium–high. Heat-load maths per PN-EN 12831 with Polish climate zones, product data maintenance per brand, accuracy liability. Cannot rely on manufacturer APIs; catalogues must be typed or scraped.
**Pros / Cons / Risks:** Pros: dated subsidy shift, simple monthly SaaS, easy to specify the maths, Polish-first. Cons: manufacturers give sizing tools away free, foreign analogues are cheap (£35), accuracy errors blamed on the tool, seasonality. Risks: wrong sizing liability; subsidy rule churn; heat-pump market stagnation; catalogue upkeep.

**Cheapest validation test (≤1 week, ≤200 PLN):** Build a clickable calculator plus a sample offer PDF; send to 40 small installers (Google Maps, e-mail) asking "would you pay 149 PLN/month?" and to run one real house through it. Success: 8 replies, 3 run a real house, 1 pre-orders.
**Kill criteria:** Fewer than 3 of 40 installers reply; or installers say they use the manufacturer's tool free and do not need another.
**Confidence:** low
