# 023 — Shift scheduling for security-guard firms
**One-liner:** A scheduling and hours tool built for Polish physical-security companies: schedules per protected site and contract, 12-hour shifts under the "system równoważny", rest-period checks, mobile clock-in at the post and a monthly hours report per client for invoicing. Research found general Polish schedulers with rest-rule checks and two guard-specific tools already on the Polish market, and no complaint evidence.
**Field:** hr-payroll | **Type:** SaaS
**Target customer:** Owners and dispatchers of private security companies with 20–300 guards. Market size: one scientific paper (date not given in the search result) cites a figure attributed to A. Czop of 5,200 security firms employing 114 thousand qualified protection workers and about 300 thousand people in private security in total (via search summary, old and undated, unverified for 2026). Current count from MSWiA or Polska Izba Ochrony: not found.
**Problem:** A guard company schedules the same people across many client sites, uses 12-hour shifts and must keep daily and weekly rest (11 h and 35 h) while billing each client for covered hours. Tools in general (Kadromierz, inEwi) check labour-law rules but do not describe site or contract billing (see Competition).
**MVP scope (≤6 weeks):** (1) Sites and contracts with required posts per hour. (2) Schedule grid by guard and post with conflict, rest and overtime warnings. (3) Mobile web clock-in per post with optional GPS. (4) Monthly hours export per client and per employee for payroll. (5) Stripe subscription per guard. Not in MVP: patrol tour checkpoints, alarm integrations, licence tracking, payroll calculation.
**Why now:** No dated trigger found for this sector. Generic: Polish working-time rules are stable (unverified in detail), and no new law was identified.
**Foreign analogues:** Papershift (Germany): shift planning at 57.60 / 86.40 / 129.60 EUR per month for a default 16 employees on annual billing plus a 39 EUR base fee; has a page for security companies but names no sector-specific features; no customer count (own pricing page). ShiftFlow (company origin not stated): security-guard time tracking from mobile at assigned posts with GPS, reports per client and contract, 14-day trial, "over 10,000" reviews with a 4.8 rating (own page, self-reported; price on a separate page, not read). No revenue figures found (unverified).
**Competition (PL / global):** Kadromierz: 12.99 / 15.99 / 19.99 PLN net per employee per month, schedule generation, a check against labour-law rules, shift swaps, absence tracking, no mention of 12-hour shifts or rest rules (own page). inEwi: schedules plus time recording, supports working-time systems including "równoważny" and warns of daily-norm risks (vendor roundup). HRappka, Symfonia roundups. Anolla (Polish page "Oprogramowanie do rezerwacji ochroniarzy"): security posts, teams, patrol routes, contract clients, alarm-system integrations, free account (own page; pricing not stated). Gap: guard-company-specific features exist at Anolla and ShiftFlow; Polish rest-rule depth versus contract billing is unproven.
**Evidence of pain:** None found: no Polish forum, group or review complaint from security companies about scheduling tools was found (searches 2026-10-29; Facebook groups and Elektroda threads not readable or old). Search trend unreadable (unverified). Lens 6 scored 1.
**Monetization:** Per guard per month: 14 PLN net up to 50 guards, 12 PLN above (estimate, matching Kadromierz 13–20 PLN), plus 3 PLN for the client-billing report. Payer: owner of the security company. Monthly, scales with headcount.
**Revenue sketch (estimate):** Assumes 15 PLN per guard. 6 months: 4 firms × 40 guards = 160 guards = 2,400 PLN MRR. 12 months: 10 firms × 50 guards = 500 guards = 7,500 PLN MRR. 10,000 PLN MRR needs about 670 guards, about 13 firms of 50: a small customer count, but each sale means replacing Excel or a general tool, and how many firms do that is unverified.
**First 10 customers:** Direct outreach to security companies (the CEIDG/KRS PKD 80.10.Z segment (code from memory, verify); consent rules in L8-F13 to F15), Polska Izba Ochrony newsletters (existence unverified), a free schedule audit for 20-guard firms.
**Build complexity:** low–medium. Risks: correct working-time rules (equivalent system, reference periods) are easy to get wrong and need legal review; no regulatory licence for software itself.
**Pros / Cons / Risks:** Pros: per-seat pricing with a small number of customers needed, a narrow listable vertical, easy to test automatically. Cons: guard-specific competitors already exist (Anolla, ShiftFlow), general tools check rest rules, no pain evidence, long sales cycle for small firms. Risks: working-time compliance errors cause customer liability; seasonality of contracts; low willingness to pay of small agencies (unverified).
**Scores (1–5):** L1 2, L2 1, L3 4, L4 2, L5 1, L6 1, L7 4, L8 3, L9 3 | **Weighted:** 2.4 / 5 (computed 2.41) | **Chance band:** <5% (T16 review: L2 2→1, see Review changes; L6 = 1 knock-out; write-up value was 2.5 (computed 2.53), <5%; shortlist 2.5)
**Cheapest validation test (≤1 week, ≤200 PLN):** Phone 20 security companies (found through CEIDG or Google Maps), ask how they build 12-hour schedules and bill client hours, and offer to build next month's schedule in a prototype for free. Success: 5 of 20 describe a clear pain, 2 agree to a pilot at 15 PLN per guard.
**Kill criteria:** Most firms say Excel or an existing system works; or no firm cites contract billing as the pain; or no one agrees to pay above 10 PLN per guard.
**Confidence:** low
**Devil's advocate:** The write-up's distinctive features (posts per site, contract clients, clock-in at the post, hours per client) are already on Polish-language pages of two guard-specific products. A second search for Polish security-company software returned exactly those two first: Anolla (security posts, teams, patrol routes, contract clients, access and monitoring integrations, free account) and ShiftFlow (clock-in at assigned posts with GPS or geofencing, dashboards of attendance and staffing across sites, reports per client and contract). C-Guard Pro (English) adds checkpoint scanning, incident reports and a client portal (Capterra listing via search summary; page 403). Kadromierz at 13–20 PLN per employee and inEwi already check labour-law rules and support the równoważny system. What is left is "Polish rest-rule depth plus contract billing", which no firm was found asking for in three searches (English, two Polish). The sector competes on thin margins and hourly-rate contracts, so a per-guard fee is a direct cost the owner weighs against an Excel sheet; the firm count (5,200) is old and undated. There is no trigger (L5 = 1), and the buyers are reached by phone or field sales, which is slow at ~10 h/week.
**Review changes:** L2 2→1 (two guard-specific tools in Polish, one with a free account, plus general schedulers with the same rest checks; no reason to switch), weighted 2.5 (computed 2.53)→2.4, band stays <5%. Rejected: no pain evidence (L6 = 1 knock-out), no trigger, and the claimed vertical features already exist; logged in rejected.md.
