# 014 — Minutes and uchwała drafts from a wspólnota meeting recording
**One-liner:** A property manager (zarządca) uploads the recording of an owners' meeting; the tool returns a Polish protocol draft, a list of uchwały with vote counts by share (udziały), and the written notice text for owners who vote individually.
**Field:** real-estate-construction | **Type:** SaaS
**Target customer:** Professional managers of wspólnoty mieszkaniowe (zarządcy) and self-managing boards of larger wspólnoty. Count: unverified; no official count of wspólnoty or zarządcy found.
**Problem:** Yearly meetings must be called at least once a year (first quarter), with written notice at least a week before; resolutions pass by majority of all owners counted by udziały, and votes can be collected at the meeting or individually, in which case each owner must be notified in writing of the resolution (poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl summary of the ustawa o własności lokali). Writing protocol, resolutions and vote tallies afterwards is clerical work. Whether the statute requires a protocol, and in what form, I could not verify (searches returned no statutory text); treat as unverified and check before building.
**MVP scope (≤6 weeks):** (1) Upload audio (or paste a transcript); speech-to-text in Polish; (2) LLM extraction of agenda items, motions, and outcomes into a protocol template; (3) vote ledger: owner list with udziały (CSV import), votes at the meeting and individual votes collected later, running majority by share; (4) generate the individual-voting notice letter; (5) per-wspólnota pricing, Stripe. Not in MVP: legal opinions, notarial protocols (a change of management method needs a notary protocol per a Poznań court decision cited in a search summary), e-voting identity.
**Why now:** Cheap speech-to-text ($0.003 per minute for gpt-4o-mini-transcribe, L5-F25) and schema output (L5-F28) make a long recording cheap to process: a two-hour meeting is about 0.36 USD (own arithmetic). No regulatory trigger.
**Foreign analogues:** MinuteSmith (turns notes, transcripts or recordings into formatted minutes for HOAs and condo associations; price not found; GetApp page returned 403). SMPLR HOA (Capterra lists from $49 per user per month; says AI generates structured minutes; page returned 403 to the fetcher, facts from a search summary). Traction numbers: none found for any.
**Competition (PL / global):** No Polish wspólnota-specific minutes tool found (searches in Polish, 2026-10-29), but generic Polish-language AI note-takers exist (Notta, HappyScribe Polish pages, found by search; not priced here). Property software: Moja Wspólnota (99–1,049 PLN/month plus per-unit fee, L3-F34) lists only electronic voting on selected resolutions (since Sep 2017) and no meeting-minutes feature on its home page; owner not named. Pro Virtus (desktop, 2,098–2,798 PLN one-off, L3-F34) and eMieszkaniec (499 PLN/year) cover ledger and communication. Gap: the meeting document workflow; unproven demand.
**Evidence of pain:** None found. No manager, wspólnota member or forum thread complaining about minutes or uchwały drafting was read. A vendor-style tip says to transcribe with Teams or Otter and prompt a chatbot (flowhunt.io), which shows DIY workarounds exist but not that managers want to pay. Privacy warning from a vendor: some AI transcribers train on transcripts, a risk for resident data. Search-trend data: unreadable; unverified.
**Monetization:** 15–29 PLN per wspólnota per meeting, or 5 PLN per month per 100 units via annual plan (estimates). Payer: zarządca. Anchor: Moja Wspólnota charges about 199–399 PLN/month for a 200–500 unit manager (L3-F34), so a 99 PLN/month add-on for a manager with 20 wspólnoty is plausible but untested.
**Revenue sketch (estimate):** Assumes 99 PLN average per manager per month. 6 months: 20 managers = 1,980 PLN MRR. 12 months: 60 managers = 5,940 PLN MRR. 10,000 PLN MRR needs about 101 managers; manager count unverified, and usage is concentrated in Q1–Q2 (seasonality), so annual billing is needed.
**First 10 customers:** List 100 managers from property-manager association and Google Maps pages (listable); offer free minutes for the next meeting in exchange for a testimonial.
**Build complexity:** medium. Polish speech accuracy with many speakers in a room, vote arithmetic by udziały, legal-form correctness of drafts, GDPR (resident names, recordings).
**Pros / Cons / Risks:** Pros: clear time saving per meeting; per-manager B2B; buildable with commodity APIs. Cons: seasonal use (annual meetings in Q1), unclear pain, recordings of meetings in noisy rooms, legal-drafting liability. Risks: statutory form requirements (unverified), Polish ASR quality, privacy of residents, Moja Wspólnota adding a feature.
**Scores (1–5):** L1 2, L2 3, L3 3, L4 2, L5 3, L6 1, L7 3, L8 3, L9 2 | **Weighted:** 2.4 / 5 (computed 2.44) | **Chance band:** <5% (T16 review: L1 3→2, L6 2→1, L9 3→2, see Review changes; L6 = 1 is a knock-out to <5%; write-up value was 2.8 (computed 2.77), <5%; shortlist 2.9)
**Cheapest validation test (≤1 week, ≤200 PLN):** Record or obtain 3 real meeting recordings from volunteer managers, produce protocol drafts with off-the-shelf APIs, ask 10 managers to review the draft and say what they would pay per meeting. Success: 3 managers say the draft saves ≥1 hour and one pays for the next meeting.
**Kill criteria:** Fewer than 3 of 15 managers record meetings or say writing the protocol takes more than 30 minutes; or a lawyer-confirmed form requirement makes AI drafts unusable.
**Confidence:** low
**Devil's advocate:** The product is used about once a year per wspólnota. The statute requires one annual meeting in Q1, and many resolutions are collected individually rather than voted in a room, so there is often no recording to process; a manager with 20 wspólnoty has a few busy weeks and then nothing, which makes monthly billing churn and annual billing a hard sell for an unproven tool. The legal exposure is larger than the write-up says: the Supreme Court held (III CZP 59/19, 27 Feb 2020) that a resolution adopted by individual vote collection can be annulled if not every owner was given a chance to vote and that could have affected the outcome, and a commentary site warns that a missing protocol with vote results can lead to a successful challenge under art. 25 u.w.l. A tool that keeps the vote ledger and drafts the notice letters becomes part of the evidence in such disputes, so a careful manager will keep doing it by hand or with the property software that already holds the owner list and udziały (Moja Wspólnota has had e-voting on resolutions since 2017). The time saving is a protocol draft that a cheap generic Polish note-taker (Notta, HappyScribe) or a chatbot prompt (the DIY guide in the sources) already gets most of the way to. A second Polish search for wspólnota software with protocols and online voting turned up legal commentary and resolution templates, no new vendor, and no manager complaining; the foreign analogues show no traction either.
**Review changes:** L1 3→2 (the two foreign analogues show no reviews, customer counts or revenue, so "visible traction" for 3 is not met), L6 2→1 (the write-up found no complaint or request at all; the rubric's 1 is "no one is found complaining", and a second search found none; this is a knock-out to <5%), L9 3→2 (once-a-year use per wspólnota means seasonal churn, generic note-takers cap the price, and the incumbent holding owner lists can add the feature), weighted 2.8 (computed 2.77)→2.4, band stays <5%. Not rejected: no direct Polish competitor was found and the validation test (3 recordings, 10 managers) is cheap, but it is a weak candidate.
