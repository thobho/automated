## Patterns (Germany)
1. **The adviser stays in the loop.** mika (Steuerberater partner network), Limetax (firm-staff review layer), Skalar (AI-native tax firm, 100+ clients per expert), Taxfix (expert filing), JUPUS (lawyer approves). German regulation of tax and legal advice (StBerG, RDG) forces AI-plus-licensed-human, exactly as in the UK. Poland's ustawa o doradztwie podatkowym and biuro rachunkowe structure produce the same shape.
2. **DATEV is the platform, not the competitor.** Limetax builds on DATEV; Candis sells a licensed DATEV interface. In PL the analogue integration targets are Comarch, Symfonia, enova365, InsERT (see [local-moats.md](../../poland/local-moats.md) section I).
3. **Platforms absorb point AI.** Doctolib bought Aaron.ai; Wolters Kluwer bought Libra and took it into Poland within three months. Point tools on top of a dominant platform (ZnanyLekarz, LEX) carry acquisition upside and absorption risk.
4. **AI roll-ups of fragmented service firms** (Limetax for tax firms, Buena for property managers) are the most heavily funded model. They need capital the founder doesn't have; the software layer they build is what can be sold to Polish biura and zarządcy instead.
5. **Benefit-form wizards sell without AI** (Elterngeld Buddy, formie). A real LLM layer (reading payslips, explaining decisions, appeals) would be a step up.
