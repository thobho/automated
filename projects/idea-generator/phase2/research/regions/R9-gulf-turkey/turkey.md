Context: Turkey's AI funding is small in money terms. A Presidential Investment Office figure cited in a LinkedIn post (excerpt) gives USD 36.4M across 81 AI rounds in 2025; Ekonomist gives USD 269.4M for 63 AI startups in 2024 ([Ekonomist](https://www.ekonomist.com.tr/makale/yapay-zeka-pazari-1-milyar-dolara-ulasacak-56045), excerpt). The two definitions clearly differ. Two kinds of AI company stand out: **Istanbul consumer app studios** (Codeway, FERASET) that sell global subscription apps, and a new crop of **legal AI on the Turkish case-law corpus** (Jupytr, Apilex, Safahat, Leagle). Many Turkish-founded B2B companies sell mainly in the US or UK (Turbo Law, Bolt Insight, Jotform).

## Patterns (Turkey)
1. **National case-law legal AI is crowded within one year**: Jupytr, Apilex, Safahat, Leagle, all built on a 10–11M-decision Turkish corpus, priced per seat in TL. Jupytr reports firms moving from Harvey/Legora to the local product: the local corpus and local formats beat the global leader. Apilex now plans DE/FR/ES/IT. This is the strongest R9 signal for a Polish legal research product on SAOS and ISAP.
2. **Teacher paperwork is a paid consumer category** (Öğretmen Evrak, 5,606 ratings, still top-grossing five years on). The same pattern is even stronger in Saudi Arabia (see [saudi-arabia.md](saudi-arabia.md)).
3. **Istanbul app studios** (Codeway, FERASET) make global AI wrapper apps funded by paid acquisition. This is a hype marker, not an adaptation target.
4. **Turkish founders build for the US and UK** (Turbo Law, Bolt Insight, Jotform): not country-specific.
