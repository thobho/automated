# Friction patterns: why Oponeo, Uber and Deliveroo won

T03 of Phase 3 ([task](../../tasks/03-friction-patterns.md), [brief](../../BRIEF.md)). 22 cases of businesses that won by removing friction when ordering something, then a typology of winning patterns, a section on what cheap AI changes, and a graveyard. All sources accessed **2026-11-06** unless noted. Friction types use the brief's six: **Finding, Fit, Trust, Logistics, Coordination, Price discovery**.

Reading notes:
- "Public numbers" are what a source states; anything I computed says "computed", anything I could not confirm says "unverified".
- Several facts about Oponeo, Autodoc, Allegro, Ceneo and Booksy are reused from [research/poland](../poland/) (T02) and point to the finding there.
- "Time until it worked" is often not public. Where it is not, the row says so instead of guessing.

## 1. Cases

### A. Vehicle: tyres and parts

**1. Oponeo (Poland, tyres, later bikes via Dadelo)**
- Friction: Fit (which tyre size and load index), Logistics (tyres are bulky and must be fitted), Price discovery.
- Trick: search by car or by tyre size, plus **delivery straight to a partner fitting workshop**, so the buyer orders and books fitting in one step. Strefa Inwestorów says Oponeo pioneered the "delivery plus fitting" service in Poland and offers it in almost 1,100 points in Poland and over 6,000 across all its markets (date of the figure not given). Autocentrum also gives "over a thousand" Polish partner workshops.
- Earns: retail margin on tyres and wheels (it holds stock); revenue 2025 PLN 2,370.9 m, net profit PLN 58.4 m, net margin about 2.5% (computed), see [ecommerce.md](../poland/ecommerce.md) E10.
- First customers / time: not found in a primary source (unverified).
- Lesson: the fitting network is the moat that a pure web shop lacks, but the business is a thin-margin, inventory-heavy retailer: the pattern, not the model, is what the founder can copy.
- Sources: [Strefa Inwestorów, Oponeo.pl SA profile](https://strefainwestorow.pl/notowania/spolki/PLOPNPL00013); [Autocentrum, "Nie stój w kolejkach…"](https://www.autocentrum.pl/publikacje/porady/nie-stoj-w-kolejkach-badz-mobilny-przy-wymianie-opon/); [BiznesRadar, Oponeo 2025 results](https://www.biznesradar.pl/a/146110,grupa-oponeo-pl-podsumowanie-wynikow-za-2025-rok).

**2. Blackcircles.com (UK, tyres with fitting; bought by Michelin)**
- Friction: Logistics, Coordination (getting tyres to a fitter at a set time), Price discovery.
- Trick: buy tyres online, **pick a local independent garage from a partner network**, tyres are delivered there for fitting. Reported network: 1,350 independent garages at the time of the sale.
- Earns: retail margin on tyres; fitting price set with the garage.
- Public numbers: revenue £28 m in 2013; Q1 2015 growth +34% year on year; Michelin bought it for £50 m in May 2015 (founder Mike Welch kept a stake and stayed). Michelin bought a 40% stake in the French online tyre seller Allopneus in the same period.
- Lesson: same pattern as Oponeo; the network of independent fitters was valuable enough for a manufacturer to buy it.
- Sources: [AM Online, 7 May 2015](https://am-online.com/news/aftersales/2015/05/07/50m-sale-of-online-tyre-retailer-blackcircles-makes-founder-a-millionaire); [Fleet News, 7 May 2015](https://www.fleetnews.co.uk/news/latest-fleet-news/2015/05/07/michelin-buys-blackcirclescom-for-50-million).

**3. Autodoc (Germany, car parts across Europe)**
- Friction: Finding and Fit (which part fits this car), Price discovery.
- Trick: a pan-European catalogue searched by car model, low prices, central logistics.
- Earns: retail margin; gross profit about 42% of revenue (computed). Also a B2B arm (AUTODOC PRO, over EUR 140 m) and a third-party marketplace with 400+ sellers in 10 countries. Runs affiliate programmes that pay more for new than for returning customers ([monetisation.md](../poland/monetisation.md) M4, rates unverified).
- Public numbers: 2025 revenue EUR 1,807.9 m (+16.3%), 9.3 m active customers, 18.9 m orders, 27 countries ([ecommerce.md](../poland/ecommerce.md) E11).
- First customers / time: not checked in a primary source (unverified).
- Sources: [AUTODOC 2025 results, EQS via Deutsche Börse](https://live.deutsche-boerse.com/news/EQS-News-AUTODOC-solidifies-European-leadership-with-strong-2025-performance-c76106a2-1307-4f01-9eb0-de73003f14c7).

**4. Autofixer (Poland, car parts platform, founded 2021)**
- Friction: Finding and Fit (helping drivers find parts that fit their car).
- Trick: an online parts platform; details of the matching method not checked.
- Earns: sale of parts (model details unverified).
- Public numbers (company statements, not independently verified): about EUR 41 m revenue in the first ten months of 2026, about EUR 52 m expected for the year, positive monthly EBITDA since July 2026; EUR 3 m venture debt from Orbit Capital (Oct 2026); next market Czechia. HQ in Łuków.
- Time until it worked: founded 2021, EBITDA-positive months reported five years later.
- Lesson: a Polish newcomer could still grow a parts business next to Inter Cars, Autodoc and Allegro in 2021–2026. Shows the Polish parts market is not closed, but the scale reached needs capital (debt financing).
- Sources: [tech.eu, 28 Oct 2026](https://tech.eu/2026/10/28/autofixer-receives-eur3m-from-orbit-capital-to-support-european-expansion/); [Vestbee](https://vestbee.com/insights/articles/autofixer-lands-3-m).

**5. RockAuto (USA, car parts, family business since 1999)**
- Friction: Finding, Fit, Price discovery.
- Trick: a plain, fast **catalogue drilled down by make, year, model and engine**, showing many brands and price levels side by side for the same part. Founders' story (as retold): a family of automotive engineers tired of searching for parts built one catalogue across many brands.
- Earns: retail margin; ships from many warehouses (reported, not verified).
- First customers: grew mostly by word of mouth among DIY mechanics and enthusiast forums (VP Tom Taylor interviews; the detail is from trade-press summaries).
- Public numbers: revenue not public (unverified).
- Lesson: a catalogue that is boring but complete and correct beats a pretty shop; price comparison inside one shop removes the need to go elsewhere.
- Sources: [AftermarketNews, "Tom Taylor: How Rock Auto Grew to be A DIYer's 1st Call"](https://www.aftermarketnews.com/?p=218285); [Diesel Tech, "Rock and roll auto parts" (2018)](https://www.dieseltechmag.com/2018/06/rock-and-roll-auto-parts).

### B. Home and appliance parts

**6. PartSelect (USA and Canada, appliance spare parts)**
- Friction: Finding (the buyer knows the fridge, not the part number) and Fit.
- Trick: **search by the full model number from the appliance's label**, then pick the part from exploded diagrams; repair videos and help. The FAQ says you need the complete, exact model number to find the right part. It sells OEM parts and states a catalogue of two million parts.
- Earns: retail margin on parts.
- History (from PartSelect's own pages, quoted by search; the History page blocks the fetch tool): 25 years in 2024 (so started about 1999); parent Eldis Group in the trade since 1945 (unverified).
- Lesson: the model number on the rating plate is the key that turns "my dishwasher leaks" into one SKU. A Polish copy needs the same model-to-part data for brands sold in Poland.
- Sources: [PartSelect model number FAQ](https://partselect.com/model-number-faq); [PartSelect help page](https://www.partselect.com/assets/html/help.html); [PartSelect "Two Million Parts"](https://PartSelect.com/Two-Million-Parts.htm); [PartSelect history](https://www.partselect.com/About/History/) (blocks bots).

**7. Partly (New Zealand, parts data for the automotive trade, B2B)**
- Friction: Finding and Fit, but sold to the trade (parts sellers, repairers), not to drivers.
- Trick: turn manufacturer catalogues into one structured parts database; a specialised model ("Interpreter") trained on licensed manufacturer data, proprietary catalogues, human feedback and synthetic data.
- Earns: software and data for businesses (pricing not public).
- Public numbers: founded 2020; NZ$37 m Series A (Dec 2022); USD 50 m Series B at a USD 500 m valuation, led by DST Global (June 2026); US office in Austin.
- Time: about six years from founding to the Series B.
- Lesson: compatibility data is itself a business. Fitment data is the hard, valuable layer; AI is used to build and clean it, with licensed data as the base.
- Sources: [SiliconANGLE, 23 Jun 2026](https://siliconangle.com/2026/06/23/partly-raises-50m-500m-valuation-crack-us-auto-parts-market/); [Startup Daily, Series A](https://www.startupdaily.net/topic/funding/kiwi-car-parts-startup-partly-banks-record-nz37-million-series/).

### C. Professional and MRO buying

**8. MonotaRO (Japan, MRO supplies for small firms)**
- Friction: Finding (tens of millions of odd items), Logistics (fast delivery of one item), Price discovery (no need to negotiate with a local dealer).
- Trick: a huge online catalogue for small factories and workshops that used to buy from local dealers by phone, with a large share in stock for same-day shipping.
- Earns: retail margin.
- Public numbers (company Q4 FY2025 deck): non-consolidated sales JPY 322.8 bn in 2025 (JPY 276.1 bn in 2024); over 28.85 m SKUs, of which 755 k ship same day; registered customer accounts about 11.3 m at end of 2025 (from a summary of the deck).
- Lesson: small professional buyers suffer the same finding friction as consumers but buy again and again; this is where a repeat model works.
- Source: [MonotaRO FY2025 Q4 presentation](https://corp.monotaro.com/en/ir/upload_file/m004-m004_01/MonotaRO2025_Q4_ENG.pdf).

**9. McMaster-Carr (USA, industrial supplies)**
- Friction: Finding (exact technical item), Logistics (needed tomorrow).
- Trick: a famously precise catalogue with CAD drawings and specs, products shipped from stock and delivered next day; targeted at maintenance and engineering buyers who need "onesies and twosies" that manufacturers will not sell singly.
- Earns: margin at roughly list price; the Harvard case argues the service supports higher margins.
- Public numbers: private; trade press reports ~700,000 products and 98% same- or next-day delivery (secondary, unverified); revenue only as third-party estimates.
- Lesson: buyers pay full price for certainty and speed when downtime costs more than the part.
- Source: [Harvard Digital Initiative (RC-TOM), "McMaster-Carr – Delivering Supplies and Service"](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-rctom/submission/mcmaster-carr-delivering-supplies-and-service/).

### D. On-demand coordination: rides, food, parcels

**10. Uber (USA, rides, later delivery)**
- Friction: Coordination (finding a car now), Trust (rating, known price), Price discovery (fare shown up front).
- Trick: summon a car with one button; GPS matching; payment stored in the app, so no cash and no haggling.
- First customers: UberCab launched in San Francisco in mid-2010 as a black-car service costing about 1.5x a taxi; within two weeks of the App Store listing about 10 drivers were doing more than 10 rides per weekend night (per Business Insider / LoveMoney summaries).
- Earns: take rate on gross bookings (commission and fees).
- Public numbers (FY2025): gross bookings USD 193.5 bn (+19%), 13.6 bn trips (+20%), 202 m monthly active platform consumers in Q4 2025.
- Time: fifteen years; years of losses and subsidies, which the brief counts as a hype warning for any small copy.
- Sources: [Uber Q4 and FY2025 results, Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260204947622/en/); [Business Insider, "Uber turns five"](https://www.businessinsider.in/tech/uber-turns-five-today-heres-how-it-took-over-the-world-in-just-a-few-years/slidelist/47507604.cms).

**11. Deliveroo (UK, restaurant delivery)**
- Friction: Logistics (good restaurants that did not deliver), Coordination.
- Trick: supply the delivery fleet to restaurants that had none, starting with better restaurants than the takeaway apps of the time.
- First customers: co-founder Will Shu delivered orders himself, by his own account full-time as a rider for about the first year (2013), often to friends and ex-colleagues.
- Earns: commission from restaurants plus delivery and service fees from customers.
- Outcome: bought by DoorDash; completed 2 October 2025 at 180p per share; DoorDash's 10-K states purchase consideration of USD 3,724 m.
- Lesson: doing the physical step by hand at the start taught the operation; the business only worked at scale and after a long loss-making period.
- Sources: [Yahoo Finance, "DoorDash finalises $3.8bn takeover of Deliveroo"](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/doordash-finalises-3-8bn-takeover-113850444.html); [DoorDash FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000179278926000013/dash-20251231.htm); [Entrepreneur Middle East, interview with Will Shu](https://www.entrepreneur.com/article/307544).

**12. Wolt (Finland, delivery; active in Poland)**
- Friction: Logistics, Coordination; also groceries and retail.
- Trick: polished app and courier network in medium-sized European cities that large players served badly.
- Outcome: DoorDash completed the all-stock acquisition on 1 June 2022 (announced Nov 2021 at about USD 8 bn); DoorDash then operated in 27 countries, 23 of them under the Wolt brand.
- Lesson: on-demand delivery is a scale game; the winners are consolidated into global groups. Not a pattern for a solo founder, but Wolt's courier network is a possible **logistics partner** for a local "last step".
- Source: [Wolt press release, "DoorDash completes acquisition of Wolt"](https://press.wolt.com/en-WW/215041-doordash-completes-acquisition-of-wolt).

**13. InPost (Poland, parcel lockers)**
- Friction: Logistics (waiting at home for a courier; returns).
- Trick: 24/7 automated parcel machines near home, cheap for shippers, plus easy returns.
- Earns: per-parcel fees from merchants and senders.
- Public numbers (FY2025): 763.1 m parcels in Poland (+8%), group 1,364.8 m (+25%); over 28,000 lockers in Poland (+11%), 61,196 lockers group-wide.
- Lesson for the founder: the Polish "last mile" is solved and cheap; a new business should build on lockers, not compete with them. Parcel prices for small senders are in [logistics-and-returns.md](../poland/logistics-and-returns.md).
- Source: [InPost FY2025 press release](https://inpost.eu/sites/default/files/2026-03/InPost%20FY%202025%20Press%20Release_0.pdf).

### E. Fit and returns in fashion

**14. Zalando (Germany, fashion; active in Poland)**
- Friction: Fit (size), Trust (returns), Logistics.
- Trick: free delivery and free, long returns removed the risk of buying clothes unseen; later size advice. Coverage of the 2025 results says AI-driven size and fit tools cut size-related returns by more than 8%.
- Earns: retail margin plus partner programme and advertising.
- Public numbers (2025): GMV EUR 17.6 bn (+14.7%), revenue EUR 12.3 bn (+16.8%), adjusted EBIT EUR 591 m (4.8% margin), 62.0 m active customers (includes ABOUT YOU from July 2025).
- Lesson: generous returns win buyers but cost money; a small founder cannot fund free returns, so fit must be solved **before** the order.
- Sources: [Zalando Annual Report 2025](https://corporate.zalando.com/sites/default/files/media-download/zalando-se_annual-report-2025_eng_261003_s.pdf); [Ecotextile, "Zalando reports record annual results"](https://www.ecotextile.com/2026031262026/radar/zalando-reports-record-annual-results/).

**15. Fit Analytics (Germany, size advice for shops; bought by Snap)**
- Friction: Fit.
- Trick: "Fit Finder" asks the shopper for height, weight and a few questions and combines this with machine learning to recommend a size; sold to retailers as a widget.
- Earns: B2B software fees from shops.
- Outcome: Snap acquired it in March 2021 (price not disclosed); over 100 staff joined Snap in Berlin.
- Lesson: a fit-answer layer can be sold to shops instead of building a shop; this is the "sell the matching, not the goods" variant.
- Source: [CNBC, 17 Mar 2021](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/03/17/snap-acquires-fit-analytics-in-e-commerce-push.html).

### F. Subscribe and save (replenishment)

**16. Chewy (USA, pet supplies)**
- Friction: Logistics (heavy bags of food), Coordination (remembering to reorder).
- Trick: **Autoship**: scheduled repeat orders with a discount, plus strong customer service.
- Earns: retail margin; Autoship gives predictable volume.
- Public numbers (FY2025 10-K): net sales USD 12.6 bn (+6.2%), Autoship customer sales USD 10.5 bn (+11.8%), reported as 83.3% of net sales. Chewy counts all sales from customers who had an Autoship order in the last 364 days, so the figure is broad.
- Lesson: replenishment of a consumable that the buyer knows by name is where "subscription" works; it is a discount-plus-convenience, not a paid search subscription.
- Sources: [Chewy 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1766502/000176650226000034/chwy-20260201.htm); [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/chewys-autoship-customer-sales-reached-83-3-of-net-sales-in-fiscal-2025).

**17. Dollar Shave Club (USA, razors by subscription)**
- Friction: Price discovery (expensive branded blades), Coordination (reordering).
- Trick: cheap blades shipped monthly; first customers from a viral launch video (widely reported; not checked here).
- Public numbers: about 3.2 m members and USD 152 m revenue in 2015; Unilever bought it in 2016 for a reported USD 1 bn (terms not disclosed by the companies).
- Lesson: subscription works for a frequent, standard consumable; it was a brand play built on marketing, not on matching.
- Source: [Fox Business, "Unilever to buy Dollar Shave Club, reportedly for $1 billion"](https://www.foxbusiness.com/markets/unilever-to-buy-dollar-shave-club-reportedly-for-1-billion).

### G. Trust in used and specialist goods

**18. Back Market (France, refurbished electronics marketplace)**
- Friction: Trust (is a used phone OK?), Price discovery.
- Trick: vetted refurbishers, graded condition, warranty and returns handled by the platform; no inventory.
- Earns: seller commission (third-party analysis says about 10% of GMV plus a monthly seller fee).
- Public numbers: GMV USD 3.5 bn in 2025 (+32%), 17 m customers in 18+ markets, EBITDA profitable globally in 2025 for the first time in its 12 years; revenue about USD 495 m is a Sacra estimate. Sacra notes customers average only 2 purchases.
- Time: about 12 years to group profitability.
- Lesson: trust can be sold as a marketplace without inventory, but low repeat purchase means acquisition cost dominates.
- Sources: [Ecommerce News Europe, "Back Market's GMV increased 32% in 2025"](https://ecommercenews.eu/back-markets-gmv-increased-32-in-2025/); [Sacra, Back Market](https://sacra.com/c/back-market/).

**19. Vinted (Lithuania, second-hand fashion; big in Poland)**
- Friction: Trust (buyer protection, pay through the platform), Logistics (prepaid labels to lockers), Price discovery.
- Trick: free listing for sellers; buyer pays a protection fee; integrated shipping labels. Its own logistics (Vinted Go) and payments (Vinted Pay) are in the 2024 release.
- Earns: buyer-protection fee, shipping, ads (fee details not checked in this run).
- Public numbers (2024): revenue EUR 813.4 m (+36%), net profit EUR 76.7 m (+330%), adjusted EBITDA EUR 158.9 m.
- Lesson: charging the **buyer** a small fee for trust and convenience works when sellers get free listing; the fee is tied to each order, not a subscription.
- Source: [Vinted newsroom, 2024 results](https://company.vinted.com/newsroom/Vinted-delivers-strong-profitable-growth-while-investing).

**20. Reverb (USA, music gear marketplace)**
- Friction: Finding (specialist and used instruments), Trust, Price discovery (price guide from past sales).
- Trick: a vertical marketplace for one hobby, with shops and individuals selling side by side, community content and price history.
- Outcome: Etsy bought it for USD 275 m in 2019 and sold it in 2025 to Creator Partners and Servco (Fender's owner), terms not disclosed. A hobby vertical can win its niche but stay small next to a horizontal parent.
- Lesson: "community plus catalogue" works for hobby gear; the exit shows the ceiling of a single-hobby vertical.
- Sources: [MusicRadar, Etsy buys Reverb](https://musicradar.com/news/reverb-sold-etsy-acquires-the-musicians-marketplace-for-dollar275-million); [Engadget, Etsy is selling Reverb](https://www.engadget.com/audio/etsy-is-selling-online-music-gear-marketplace-reverb-160002119.html).

### H. Booking and comparison in Poland

**21. Booksy (Poland-born, beauty and barber booking)**
- Friction: Coordination (booking by phone), Finding (a free slot nearby).
- Trick: booking software for the salon (calendar, reminders) that also feeds a consumer marketplace.
- Earns: salon subscription PLN 145 net/month plus 45% of a new client's first visit (cap PLN 250) for "Boost" marketplace leads ([monetisation.md](../poland/monetisation.md) M12).
- Lesson: in services, the subscription is paid by the **professional**, and the marketplace is a lead source on top of a tool they use daily.
- Source: [Booksy Biz, cennik](https://biz.booksy.com/pl-pl/cennik).

**22. Ceneo (Poland, price comparison, part of Allegro)**
- Friction: Price discovery, Trust (shop reviews).
- Trick: compare offers from many shops; shops pay per click (CPC); since 2018 a hybrid with checkout on Ceneo (secondary source).
- Earns: CPC from shops; price-comparison revenue PLN 63.7 m in Q1 2026 in Allegro's report ([ecommerce.md](../poland/ecommerce.md) E3).
- Ownership: part of the Allegro Group when Cinven, Permira and Mid Europa bought it from Naspers in 2016 for USD 3.253 bn.
- Lesson: comparison for standard items is owned by the incumbent; a newcomer has room only where items are not standard enough for Ceneo's product matching (odd parts, used items, services).
- Source: [Permira, 14 Oct 2016](https://www.permira.com/news-and-insights/news/cinven-permira-and-mid-europa-to-acquire-allegro).
