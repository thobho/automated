# Lens 3 — Clear monetization

Research for lens 3 of [BRIEF.md](../../BRIEF.md); scoring anchors in [method/criteria.md](../../method/criteria.md). All sources accessed 2026-10-29. Findings are in the topic files; IDs `L3-Fnn` are stable anchors. This lens is about **who pays, how much and how often**.

**Headline:** 31 findings (F01–F31), 23 of them read on the vendor's own pricing page. Three patterns recur across Poland, DACH, NL, IT, DK and the US:
1. **Software is cheap, the job is expensive.** Invoicing tools sell at 0–33 EUR/month (sevDesk, Lexware, Moneybird, inFakt, iFirma) while the human outcome costs 150–500 PLN/month for a JDG and 600–5,000+ PLN for a company (F01, F09–F11, F25). KSeF connectivity is now bundled free (F26). A standalone "connector" or "invoice tool" is a low-ARPU play; money sits in the outcome or in the accountant's workflow.
2. **Vertical tools for crews and professionals live in a 50–330 per month corridor** (German trades 55–330 EUR, Jobber/Housecall 60–300 USD, Shepherd 299–349 USD per vet, Time To Pet 25–79 USD) (F12, F17–F20). Prices step by users, volume (orders, conversations, pageviews, bank transactions) or professionals, with add-on modules and paid onboarding. 10,000 PLN MRR is about 30–100 customers at 100–330 PLN, which a 1 million-firm DACH niche or a 2.9 million-firm Polish base can supply (F27, F28).
3. **Law-driven compliance has visible recurring prices** (whistleblowing 79–299 EUR/month in DE, cookie/privacy 5–90 EUR/site, accessibility widgets 5–65 EUR/month with 1,500–60,000 EUR one-off remediation) (F14, F15, F24, F30), but Polish prices were not found. That is a gap in evidence, not proof of an empty market.

No finding is a complete L3 = 5 case on its own (the criteria need a named payer, 2+ competitor pricing pages and recurring monthly use *for the idea*); the pricing pages here are the input to that test for ideas in T11–T13. Several findings are explicit counter-findings (F25 shows software is not where the money sits, F26 shows a feature that is free, F31 is weak).

## Gaps
1. **Polish competitor pricing is missing for vertical niches** (trades/renovation, vet, physio, driving schools, wspólnoty, law-firm tools). Next: Polish vendor pages (e.g. Versum, Zdrowie/Medfile for clinics, Booksy competitors, FixZone-type tools) and Capterra.pl by category.
2. **Willingness-to-pay evidence from real buyers is thin.** Pricing pages show list prices, not what customers pay or churn at. Next: Indie Hackers/TrustMRR (T05), review sites, Polish forum threads ("ile płacicie za…"), founder posts with MRR and ARPU.
3. **Polish compliance prices** (whistleblowing, IOD, WCAG audits, RODO software) not found. Next: vendor sites by name (First Whistle, Sygnalizuj24h, Legiscope), Allegro/Useme gigs, quotes by email.
4. **Whistleblower DE prices are second-hand** (F14, unverified at vendors, low-reliability source). Next: hint Compliance, Whistleblower Software and BKMS own pricing pages.
5. **wFirma, Fakturownia paid-tier and Plancraft prices unreadable.** Next: a browser session or Wayback copies.
6. **Customer counts and churn per competitor are unknown.** Next: company disclosures (annual reports, KRS filings of Polish SaaS vendors), BaseLinker/Base customer numbers, LinkedIn headcounts.
7. **Market sizes are firm counts, not payer counts.** F27 uses a media article citing GUS (primary release not read); F28 uses ZDH but assumes 100% penetration for an upper bound only. Next: GUS "Zmiany strukturalne grup podmiotów" and BDL tables by PKD.
8. **Currency conversions are estimates** (4.3 PLN/EUR, 4.0 PLN/USD assumed; no FX source fetched). Recompute with NBP tables before use in write-ups.
9. **Annual versus monthly billing and net/gross are inconsistent across pages;** comparisons across vendors are approximate (F10, F11, F12, F16).
