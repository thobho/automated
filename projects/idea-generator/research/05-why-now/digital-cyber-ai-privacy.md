# AI Act, NIS2, CRA, GDPR, PSD3, accessibility

Lens 5 findings on digital, cyber and AI regulation. All sources accessed 2026-10-29. "Fetched" = page read; "search snippet" = only the search summary seen. Law-firm and trade pages are secondary; the Official Journal text was not read for any item here.

### L5-F09 — EU AI Act "Digital Omnibus": Annex III high-risk delayed to 2 Dec 2027; transparency duties (Art. 50) start 2 Aug 2026, watermarking grace to 2 Dec 2026
- **Trigger:** the Digital Omnibus on AI moved stand-alone high-risk obligations (Annex III) from 2 Aug 2026 to **2 Dec 2027**, embedded products (Annex I) to **2 Aug 2028**, public-authority AI to 2 Aug 2030. Art. 50(2) (marking AI-generated content) for generative systems placed on the market before 2 Aug 2026 gets until **2 Dec 2026**; a new Art. 5 ban on non-consensual intimate imagery ("nudifier" apps) and CSAM applies from 2 Dec 2026. A new "small mid-cap" category (under 750 employees, turnover up to 150 mln EUR or balance sheet up to 129 mln EUR) gets SME-style relief; AI-literacy duty (Art. 4, in force since 2 Feb 2025) softened.
- **Evidence:** Orrick, May 2026, https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/05/EUs-Digital-Omnibus-on-AI-7-Key-Changes-You-Need-to-Know (fetched; says the text is published and in force). Gibson Dunn's earlier page (provisional deal 6 May 2026, Council confirmation 13 May) https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/ (fetched, same dates). A search snippet dates final Council approval to 29 Jun 2026. Penalty amounts not on these pages: unverified (the AI Act text itself was not read).
- **Who:** providers and deployers of AI systems in the EU; counts not found.
- **Product category:** AI-use inventories and risk classification for SMEs, Art. 50 disclosure/labelling tooling (chatbots must say they are AI), AI-literacy training records. The delay of high-risk duties **removes** the hardest 2026 deadline: demand for "high-risk compliance" tools is pushed beyond 12 months.
- **L5 implication:** transparency/labelling duties are live now; high-risk work is a 2027 theme. Do not score high-risk-AI-compliance ideas L5=5.
- **Confidence:** medium-high on dates (two law-firm pages agree).

### L5-F10 — Poland's AI act: KRiBSI supervisor, law passed 3 Jul 2026, penalty provisions in force from 28 Oct 2026
- **Trigger:** the Polish act on AI systems creates the Komisja Rozwoju i Bezpieczeństwa Sztucznej Inteligencji (KRiBSI), which can inspect, take complaints, issue permits for high-risk systems and impose fines. Sejm passed it on 11 Jun 2026; per search snippet the Sejm accepted 24 of 25 Senate amendments on 3 Jul 2026, the President signed on 24 Jul, publication 27 Jul 2026 (Dz.U. item 1003, secondary), and provisions on supervision, proceedings and administrative fines enter into force **28 Oct 2026** (from a ministry letter to an MP, per the snippet), i.e. yesterday. At least one regulatory sandbox was to exist by 2 Aug 2026.
- **Evidence:** https://www.bankier.pl/wiadomosc/Polska-wdraza-AI-Act-Powstal-nowy-organ-nadzoru-nad-sztuczna-inteligencja-9149707.html (fetched, 11 Jun 2026: KRiBSI composition, sandbox, 14/21-day procedure periods, no fine amounts); https://samorzad.gov.pl/web/gov/ustawa-o-systemach-ai---bezpieczny-rozwoj-sztucznej-inteligencji-w-polsce (search snippet). Publication data and entry-into-force dates are from secondary sources: verify in ISAP.
- **Takeaway:** enforcement infrastructure now exists in Poland, but there is no evidence yet of inspections or fines. Maximum fine amounts: unverified.
- **Confidence:** medium-low on exact dates.

### L5-F11 — NIS2 in Poland (KSC amendment): in force 3 Apr 2026; register by 3 Oct 2026; comply by 3 Apr 2027; first audit by 3 Apr 2028
- **Trigger:** the KSC amendment implementing NIS2 took effect on 3 Apr 2026 (Clifford Chance gives 2 Apr in a search snippet; one-day difference between sources). Covered entities had to apply for the register entry by **3 Oct 2026** (passed); risk-management and security duties must be in place by **3 Apr 2027**; key entities must complete their first cybersecurity audit by **3 Apr 2028**, then every three years; administrative fines in most cases only after a two-year transition, i.e. not before April 2028. The President sent parts of the act (high-risk-vendor rules, security orders, fines) to the Constitutional Tribunal (search snippet).
- **Who:** the Ministry of Digital Affairs estimated more than 10,000 entities, against about 400 operators of essential services under the 2018 act; Rzeczpospolita writes "up to several tens of thousands". Size criterion generally medium and large companies: at least 50 employees or annual turnover above 10 mln EUR (search snippet; draft-stage estimates, no official final count found).
- **Evidence:** https://itwiz.pl/nowelizacja-ustawy-o-ksc-zaczyna-obowiazywac-rusza-harmonogram-wdrozenia-nis2-w-polsce/ (fetched: dates); https://www.cliffordchance.com/content/dam/cliffordchance/briefings/2026/03/CB-NIS2%20pol.pdf (PDF unreadable by the fetch tool; facts from the search snippet); https://www.prawo.pl/podatki/jakie-przedsiebiorstwa-obejmie-nowelizacja-ustawy-o-krajowym-systemie-cyberbezpieczenstwa,531171.html (search snippet).
- **Product category:** NIS2 readiness for mid-size Polish firms: asset/risk registers, policy templates in Polish, supplier-security questionnaires (large entities push duties down to suppliers), incident-report workflow (time limits not confirmed here), audit evidence tracking. Existing solutions and prices: not seen in this run (Gaps).
- **L5 implication:** a binding, dated trigger inside 18 months (3 Apr 2027) affecting 10,000+ medium and large firms, but with fines deferred to 2028 and the buyer typically an IT/security manager with consulting alternatives.
- **Confidence:** medium (dates from one fetched trade article + snippet; counts are estimates).

### L5-F12 — Cyber Resilience Act: vulnerability and incident reporting live since 11 Sep 2026, fines up to 15 mln EUR or 2.5% turnover; main duties from 11 Dec 2027
- **Trigger:** manufacturers of products with digital elements must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents through ENISA's Single Reporting Platform: early warning in **24 h**, notification in **72 h**, final report 14 days after a fix (vulnerabilities) or one month after the 72-h notice (incidents). Applies also to products already on the market. Most remaining duties (essential requirements, conformity assessment, CE marking) from **11 Dec 2027**. Fines up to **15 mln EUR or 2.5% of worldwide turnover**.
- **Evidence:** Jones Day, July 2026, https://www.jonesday.com/de/insights/2026/07/eu-cyber-resilience-act-24hour-reporting-duties-start-september-11-2026 (fetched). Search snippet: the platform went live on 11 Sep 2026 and the Commission issued non-binding guidance on 27 Jul 2026 (https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/data-privacy-and-cyber-risk/cyber-resilience-act-reporting-obligations-apply-from-11-september-2026, not fetched). Open-source steward treatment not verified.
- **Who:** manufacturers, importers, distributors of hardware and software with network connection placed on the EU market; counts not found.
- **Product category:** vulnerability intake and triage, SBOM generation and tracking, CRA readiness checklists and evidence packs for small software/IoT vendors. SBOM requirement from Dec 2027 is vendor-blog level only (unverified). A developer-tool seed fits the founder (Java/Node ecosystems).
- **Confidence:** medium-high on reporting dates (one fetched law-firm page + snippets).

### L5-F13 — GDPR enforcement in Poland: fines worth more than 64 mln PLN in 2025 despite fewer decisions
- **Evidence:** Grant Thornton Poland report "Kto płaci za naruszenie RODO" (Feb 2026), summarised in a search result: fewer fines than in earlier years but an unprecedented total above **64 mln PLN** in 2025 (published UODO decisions). The PDF is image-heavy and could not be read by the fetch tool, so the figure is from the search summary: https://grantthornton.pl/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Kto-placi-za-naruszenie-RODO-Grant-Thornton-2026.pdf . Largest Polish fine named in a 2026 guide: Fortum Marketing, 4.9 mln PLN (UODO, 2022); whether a larger one exists since is unverified (https://www.legiscope.com/blog/kary-administracyjne-rodo-przewodnik.html, search snippet). Example of a small-body fine: 100,000 PLN for a municipal office after a data leak (kluczesoft.pl guide, low reliability). Art. 83 caps: up to 20 mln EUR or 4% turnover.
- **Takeaway:** enforcement is steady, not a new trigger. It supports always-on products (cookie/consent, DPO tools, breach registers) but gives no deadline. L5 ceiling ≈ 3.
- **Confidence:** low-medium (summary of an unread PDF).

### L5-F14 — PSD3 / Payment Services Regulation: provisional deal 27 Nov 2025, publication and dates unconfirmed
- **Evidence (search snippets only):** provisional agreement 27 Nov 2025; compromise texts published 23 Apr 2026; ECON committee vote 5 May 2026; Official Journal publication then expected mid/late 2026; application dates reported anywhere from 18 to 27 months after entry into force; the PSR introduces refund rights when fraudsters spoof a bank and shifts the burden of proof to the payment provider; payee name/IBAN verification is already required by the Instant Payments Regulation. https://paymentexpert.com/2026/03/30/psd3-eu-payments-regulation-explained/ ; https://www.pwc.com/cz/cs/blog/rizeni-rizik/psd-3-the-psr-payments-reform.html ; https://trustpair.com/gb/?p=14892 . None read in full; no official source reached.
- **Takeaway:** relevant mostly to licensed PSPs and banks (the founder cannot hold a financial licence), so any product would be tooling for them (fraud-case workflow, name-check APIs). No deadline inside 18 months confirmed. L5 ≈ 2–3 until the Official Journal text is read.
- **Confidence:** low.

### L5-F15 — European Accessibility Act in Poland: law of 26 Apr 2024 in force 28 Jun 2025; PFRON supervises; micro service providers exempt
- **Trigger:** the Polish act (Dz.U. 2024 poz. 731) transposes Directive (EU) 2019/882. It applies from **28 Jun 2025** (already in force); supervision by the President of PFRON with sectoral regulators; fines "up to several tens of thousands of PLN" (secondary, amounts unverified); micro-enterprises providing services are excluded; disproportionate-burden exemption requires documentation.
- **Evidence:** Senate and law-firm material via search (https://eli.gov.pl/eli/DU/2024/731/ogl/pol/pdf , https://rzmlaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Alert-Polski-Akt-o-Dostepnosci.pdf , https://poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl/-wymagania-dostepnosci-produktow-i-uslug-dla-osob-z-niepelnosprawnosciami , all search snippets; one interpellation, https://api.sejm.gov.pl/sejm/term10/interpellations/18678/body , states 28 Jun 2026 and is probably an error). No enforcement action or fine was found. Price evidence for remediation and overlay widgets: [L3-F30](../03-monetization/willingness-to-pay-and-market-size.md#l3-f30--european-accessibility-act-eaa-one-off-remediation-quoted-at-150060000-eur-overlay-widgets-at-565-eurmonth-with-legal-risk-caveats).
- **Takeaway:** the deadline has passed; the live driver is enforcement, which was not found. Counts of affected e-commerce/banking/transport providers: unverified.
- **Confidence:** medium on dates, low on enforcement.
