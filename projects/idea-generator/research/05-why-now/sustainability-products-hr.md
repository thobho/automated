### L5-F16 — CSRD after the Omnibus: only companies above 1,000 employees and 450 mln EUR turnover; first reports in 2028 for FY2027
- **Trigger:** Directive (EU) 2026/470 (Omnibus I) was published in the Official Journal on 26 Feb 2026 and entered into force on 18 Mar 2026 (Deloitte says 19 Mar). CSRD scope: EU companies with more than **1,000 employees and net turnover above 450 mln EUR**; non-EU groups with EU turnover above 450 mln EUR and an EU subsidiary or branch above 200 mln EUR. Applies to financial years starting **1 Jan 2027**; first reports in 2028. Member States transpose by 19 Mar 2027 (CSRD) and 26 Jul 2028 (CSDDD, now 5,000 employees and 1.5 bn EUR). Revised ESRS due by delegated act by **18 Sep 2026** (status not checked). A cap limits what large firms may request from suppliers under 1,000 employees. Wave-1 companies falling outside the new scope may be exempted from reporting for 2026–2027 at Member State option.
- **Evidence:** Arthur Cox https://www.arthurcox.com/insights/omnibus-i-directive-published-revised-scope-and-reduced-obligations-under-csrd-and-csddd/ (fetched); thresholds, 18 Mar date, transposition dates from search snippets of https://cms.law/en/lux/legal-updates/final-adoption-of-omnibus-i-directive-simplifying-csrd-and-csddd , https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/esg/2026-posts/omnibus-i-update-csrd-and-csddd-amendment-directive-published-in-the-official-journal and https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/news/all-news/2026/feb/eu-journal-sustainability-omnibus . No page gave the number of companies leaving scope: unverified. Polish transposition status not checked.
- **Takeaway (counter-finding):** the mass-market CSRD wave for mid-size firms has been removed. The "SME ESG reporting kit" idea loses its mandate; what remains is voluntary reporting and customer questionnaires from the few thousand firms still in scope. This lowers the evidence behind seed 10 of lens 4.
- **L5 implication:** for mid-size Polish suppliers L5 should be ≤ 2 (no binding trigger).
- **Confidence:** high on thresholds (four sources agree), medium on transposition status.

### L5-F17 — CBAM definitive period since 1 Jan 2026: 50-tonne threshold, authorised-declarant status, certificates sold from 1 Feb 2027, first declaration 30 Sep 2027
- **Trigger:** importers of CBAM goods (steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, electricity, hydrogen) above a **50-tonne annual threshold** must be authorised declarants (grace for those who applied by 31 Mar 2026, now passed). Certificates for 2026 imports go on sale on **1 Feb 2027**, priced from the quarterly average of 2026 EU ETS prices; first annual declaration and surrender **30 Sep 2027**; repurchase requests by 31 Oct 2027. Sources conflict on electricity and hydrogen treatment (threshold or not): unverified.
- **Evidence (search snippets, secondary):** https://it.andersen.com/en/cbam-2026-obligations-deadlines-and-impacts-for-importers/ ; https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/10/key-changes-in-the-cbam-amendment ; https://climateaction.gov.mt/cbam/ . Regulation (EU) 2025/2083 text not read. Importer counts for Poland not found.
- **Product category:** embedded-emissions data collection from non-EU suppliers, CN-code classification, quarterly certificate cost estimator, declaration file builder for small importers. Fines and exact penalty values: not found.
- **L5 implication:** dated trigger (1 Feb 2027, 30 Sep 2027), but the payer is an importer of heavy-industry goods, a narrow B2B audience that probably already uses customs agents.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### L5-F18 — EUDR: applies 30 Dec 2026 (large and medium operators), 30 Jun 2027 (micro and small primary operators); Commission review did not reopen the text
- **Evidence (search snippet, Arendt Luxembourg summary of the 4 May 2026 simplification review):** application dates set by the December 2025 amendment; the review comprised updated guidance and FAQs, a draft delegated act refining the product scope, information-system upgrades; annual rather than per-shipment declarations possible; the Commission estimates annual compliance cost fell from about 8.1 bn EUR to 2 bn EUR (figure is the Commission's, reported second-hand). https://www.arendt.com/news-insights/news/eudr-simplification-review-published-what-the-2026-package-means-for-in-scope-companies/ ; https://dcycle.io/blog/eudr-simplification-review-2026/ . Not fetched; Official Journal text not read. Penalties and operator counts: unverified.
- **Product category:** geolocation/plot-data collection, due-diligence statement filing to the EU information system, supplier-portal. Covers wood, cocoa, coffee, soy, palm oil, rubber, cattle and derivatives (product list not verified in this run). The date is about 2 months away, which is a strong L5 signal, but the same date moved twice before (delay history seen only in search titles: unverified detail).
- **Confidence:** medium-low.

### L5-F19 — Packaging Regulation (PPWR) applies from 12 Aug 2026; labelling and some duties come later
- **Evidence (search snippets):** Regulation (EU) 2025/40 became generally applicable on **12 Aug 2026**, including PFAS limits for food-contact packaging, heavy-metal limits, manufacturer identification, technical documentation and declaration of conformity, with a new EPR "manufacturer" definition; harmonised labelling is dated 12 Aug 2028 by one source and unresolved by another; the Commission ruled out postponing to 2027. https://www.europen-packaging.eu/news/ppwr-starts-applying-from-12-august-2026-a-quick-guide-to-what-businesses-need-to-know/ ; https://www.euwid-recycling.com/news/business/european-commission-rules-out-postponing-ppwr-obligations-until-2027-211025/ ; https://www.coolset.com/academy/ppwr-compliance-deadlines-explained-what-applies-from-august-2026-and-what-comes-later . Not fetched. Polish rules (BDO registration, packaging act) were **not searched**.
- **Takeaway:** a broad trigger (every producer using packaging), but the product opportunities (packaging data, EPR reporting per country) are poorly defined in what I read. Prices of existing tools: not seen.
- **Confidence:** low-medium.

### L5-F20 — Poland's deposit-return system (system kaucyjny) started 1 Oct 2025: all shops charge the deposit, shops over 200 m² must take back containers
- **Evidence (search snippets of bank.pl and iFirma pages):** from 1 Oct 2025 deposit on plastic bottles up to 3 l, metal cans up to 1 l (50 groszy) and reusable glass bottles up to 1.5 l (1 zł; glass added in 2026 per municipal pages); all shops selling covered drinks charge the deposit; shops above 200 m² must take back empties and refund the deposit, smaller shops only reusable glass (voluntary otherwise); producers and importers join an operator organisation and pass deposits to it twice a month; the minister can withdraw operator permits and inspectors audit operators. https://bank.pl/system-kaucyjny-obowiazkowy-od-1-pazdziernika-2025-roku ; https://www.ifirma.pl/blog/system-kaucyjny-w-polsce-juz-od-1-pazdziernika-2025-r-co-musisz-wiedziec/ . Not fetched. Store counts and fines: not found.
- **Takeaway:** a live regulatory change with a working system but no product demand seen. Possible niches: reconciliation of deposit flows in POS/accounting, small-shop compliance guides. No evidence of pain gathered here (lens 6).
- **Confidence:** medium-low.

### L5-F21 — Pay Transparency Directive: transposition deadline 7 Jun 2026 missed by Poland; law expected in 2027; draft thresholds at 100 employees
- **Evidence:** Infor (article of 28 Aug 2026, fetched): Directive (EU) 2023/970 not implemented in Poland; the family ministry expects the act in force in **2027**; latest draft sent to consultation on 4 May 2026 (comments to 25 May and 3 Jun); employers with **100 or more employees** report gender pay gap metrics (gap, median gap, share of men and women per pay quartile); employers under 50 give pay-rise criteria on request; employees cannot yet invoke the directive against private employers. A linked headline mentions fines up to 60,000 PLN (not confirmed in the article body: unverified). Search snippet: the Sejm has passed an amendment to the Labour Code requiring pay information to candidates in recruitment. https://kadry.infor.pl/kadry/wynagrodzenia/ochrona_wynagrodzenia/7602279,czy-pracownik-moze-zadac-podania-wysokosci-zarobkow-kolegow-7-czerwca-2026-r-minal-termin-na-wdrozenie-dyrektywy-o-jawnosci-wynagrodzen-polska-opoznia-sie-z-ustawa.html (fetched)
- **Who:** employers with 100+ employees for reporting (count not found: Polish firm counts by size are in GUS, not read); all employers for recruitment pay ranges.
- **Product category:** pay-structure analysis from payroll exports, pay-gap report generator in Polish, job-ad pay-range checker. Other EU markets with earlier laws are not covered here (Gaps).
- **L5 implication:** binding, but the Polish date is an expectation ("2027"), not a deadline, and reporting would start later. L5 ≈ 3.
- **Confidence:** medium.
