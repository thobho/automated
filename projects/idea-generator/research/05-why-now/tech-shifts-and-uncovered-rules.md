# Tech shifts and rules not covered earlier

Lens 5 findings added in T11 gap pass. All pages accessed 2026-10-29.

Findings L5-F25 to L5-F28 are technology shifts. L5-F29 to L5-F34 are rules. Prices are list prices in USD as shown on the vendor page on the access date.

## L5-F25 — Speech-to-speech APIs got a cheap tier and phone (SIP) access (Oct 2025 – Jul 2026)
- **What:** OpenAI's API changelog lists GPT-Realtime mini (released 6 Oct 2025, "for more cost-efficient speech to speech performance"), a gpt-realtime-mini snapshot (15 Dec 2025), dedicated SIP IP ranges for the Realtime API at `sip.api.openai.com` (13 Jan 2026), GPT-Realtime-1.5 (23 Feb 2026), GPT-Realtime-2 with configurable reasoning plus GPT-Realtime-Whisper for streaming speech-to-text (7 May 2026), and GPT-Realtime-2.1 with "improved alphanumeric recognition" plus a 2.1 mini variant (6 Jul 2026). Current pricing per 1M audio tokens: gpt-realtime / 1.5 / 2 / 2.1 at $32 input, $64 output, $0.40 cached input; gpt-realtime-mini at $10 input, $20 output, $0.30 cached input. Transcription: gpt-4o-transcribe $0.006/min, gpt-4o-mini-transcribe $0.003/min. Sources: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/changelog and https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing (accessed 2026-10-29).
- **Who is affected / count:** Any small business that answers the phone (clinics, workshops, salons, property managers). Count unverified.
- **What it forces or enables:** A Polish-language phone receptionist (bookings, opening hours, order status) wired straight to a SIP trunk without a separate speech-to-text and text-to-speech stack. Alphanumeric recognition matters for reading back NIP numbers, plate numbers and order IDs. Cheap transcription ($0.003/min) makes call summaries and call-centre QA viable for very small teams.
- **Caveat:** The changelog says whisper-1 and several gpt-4o transcribe models shut down on 26 Feb 2027 (announced 26 Aug 2026), so products built on them must migrate to GPT Transcribe / GPT Live Transcribe (released 28 Jul 2026). The original gpt-realtime launch post (openai.com) returned 403, so the launch date and the previous preview-model price are unverified. No price cut is visible on the pricing page; the drop is the move to the mini tier (about 3x cheaper per token). Polish-language quality not verified.

## L5-F26 — Managed voice-agent platforms priced per minute, cut twice (Feb 2025, May 2026)
- **What:** ElevenLabs cut conversational AI call pricing to 10 cents/min on Creator and Pro plans and 8 cents/min on an annual Business plan, an "~50% discount" (post dated 11 Feb 2025); the post says these prices "do not include LLM costs". On 7 May 2026 ElevenLabs cut Agents pricing on the Starter plan from $0.10 to $0.08/min (20%), "reduced to $0.08 per minute per call across all plans", and added pay-as-you-go. Deepgram's pricing page lists a Voice Agent API at $0.075/min (Standard), $0.050/min (Custom with your own LLM and TTS), $0.163/min (Advanced), billed on WebSocket connection time; streaming STT Nova-3 Multilingual $0.0092/min, Flux Multilingual $0.0078/min. Sources: https://elevenlabs.io/blog/we-cut-our-pricing-for-conversational-ai, https://elevenlabs.io/blog/weve-lowered-api-agents-pricing-and-introduced-pay-as-you-go, https://deepgram.com/pricing (accessed 2026-10-29).
- **Who is affected / count:** Same as L5-F25, plus agencies that build bots for clients. Count unverified.
- **What it forces or enables:** At $0.05–0.08 per minute, a 3-minute call costs roughly $0.15–0.24 before LLM costs (own calculation from the listed rates). That is low enough to resell a fixed-price "AI receptionist" or overdue-invoice reminder calls to micro-firms. A solo developer can build the vertical layer (calendar, CRM, KSeF/invoice lookup) and leave telephony and voice to the platform.
- **Caveat:** The 7 May 2026 post does not say whether LLM or telephony costs are included. Deepgram's page says Nova supports "60+ languages" without listing them, so Polish support is unverified there. ElevenLabs Polish-language support was not checked on a fetched page.

## L5-F27 — LLM-based document OCR and cheap PDF input (Mar 2025 onward)
- **What:** Mistral OCR launched on 6 Mar 2025 at "1000 pages / $" (about 1,000 pages per dollar), roughly double the pages per dollar with batch, and "up to 2000 pages per minute on a single node". That launch page is now marked deprecated and replaced by newer OCR models. Current Mistral docs (`mistral-ocr-latest`) list table output as markdown or HTML, header/footer extraction (OCR 2512+), paragraph-level bounding boxes (OCR 4+), confidence scores, "more than 40 languages", PDF/PPTX/DOCX input and a `document_annotation` field for structured extraction. Google's Gemini pricing page says PDF tokens are "billed at the image token rate"; gemini-2.5-flash-lite costs $0.10 input / $0.40 output per 1M tokens ($0.05 / $0.20 batch), gemini-3.1-flash-lite $0.25 / $1.50, gemini-3.5-flash-lite $0.30 / $2.50, all with a free tier. Sources: https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-ocr, https://docs.mistral.ai/capabilities/document_ai/basic_ocr/, https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing (accessed 2026-10-29).
- **Who is affected / count:** Accounting offices (biura rachunkowe), firms that still receive paper or PDF cost invoices, receipts, delivery notes, contracts. Count unverified.
- **What it forces or enables:** Turning scanned receipts, foreign (non-KSeF) invoices, bank statements and contracts into structured JSON for bookkeeping, at well under a cent per page. Bounding boxes and confidence scores let a small product show a human review screen only for uncertain fields.
- **Caveat:** The Mistral launch date (Mar 2025) is about 19 months ago, slightly older than the 12–18 month window; the newer OCR versions' release dates and current per-page price were not on any fetched page (mistral.ai/pricing only says "OCR is per 1,000 pages"). Polish-language OCR accuracy not verified.

## L5-F28 — Schema-guaranteed JSON output is now standard, and the cheapest models cost cents per million tokens (2024–2026)
- **What:** OpenAI launched Structured Outputs on 6 Aug 2024 (outputs "reliably adhere to developer supplied JSON Schemas") and added strict tool schemas with parallel tool calls plus extra schema features (e.g. email validation, numeric and array ranges) on 20 May 2025. Anthropic's docs list structured outputs (JSON outputs via `output_config.format`, and strict tool use) as GA on the Claude API, Bedrock, Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry; "beta headers are no longer required"; the deprecated beta header is `structured-outputs-2025-11-13`. The docs say it guarantees schema-compliant responses "through constrained decoding", with exceptions for refusals, max-token cut-offs and enum casing. Cheapest listed text models: OpenAI gpt-5-nano $0.05 input / $0.40 output per 1M tokens, gpt-4.1-nano $0.10 / $0.40; Google gemini-2.5-flash-lite $0.10 / $0.40. Sources: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/changelog, https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs, https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing, https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing (accessed 2026-10-29).
- **Who is affected / count:** Developers building extraction, classification and form-filling tools. Count not applicable.
- **What it forces or enables:** Reliable "document in, fixed schema out" pipelines without retry loops: invoice fields to KSeF FA(3)-like structures, GPSR product data (see L5-F30), whistleblower report triage (see L5-F32). Combined with nano/flash-lite prices, a product can run an LLM on every incoming document or email and stay cheap.
- **Caveat:** The Anthropic page does not state a launch date; the "2025-11-13" in the beta header name suggests November 2025 but that is an inference, unverified. OpenAI's Structured Outputs launch (Aug 2024) is older than 18 months; the newer part is strict-mode coverage and Anthropic GA.

## L5-F29 — EU Data Act: applies since 12 Sep 2025, access-by-design from 12 Sep 2026, no cloud switching charges from 12 Jan 2027
- **What:** The Commission says the Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854) "is applicable from 12 September 2025". The access-by-design duty in Art. 3(1) applies to connected products and related services newly placed on the market from 12 Sep 2026 (Heuking). Germany's Bundesnetzagentur says that during the transition providers "may only charge costs that are directly linked to the switching process" and "From 12 January 2027, no switching charges at all may be imposed (Article 29(1) of the Data Act)". Sources: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/data-act, https://www.heuking.de/en/news-events/newsletter-articles/detail/data-act-access-by-design-becomes-mandatory-as-of-12-september-what-companies-need-to-know-now.html, https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/SharedDocs/A_Z_Glossar_DataAct_ENG/S/switching-charge.html (accessed 2026-10-29).
- **Who is affected / count:** Per the Commission page: manufacturers of connected devices sold in the EU, users of those devices, public sector bodies, cloud and data-processing service providers (which includes SaaS, per law-firm search summaries), and companies with data-sharing contracts. Heuking adds that Art. 1(4) may reach providers of virtual assistants. Count unverified.
- **What it forces or enables:** (1) Small IoT makers need a user data-access portal or API and an export in "machine-readable format" for products placed on the market after 12 Sep 2026 (wording from a search summary). (2) Every SaaS vendor, including small Polish ones, needs a switching/export process and contract terms by 12 Jan 2027 — a "Data Act switching kit" (export tooling, contract clauses, pre-contract information under Art. 29(4)). (3) Data-sharing request handling for device users.
- **Caveat:** EUR-Lex was not readable (bot challenge), so the regulation text itself was not read. Bundesnetzagentur gives both 11 and 12 Jan 2027 as the end of the transition on the same page; law-firm search summaries also differ (11 vs 12 Jan 2027). The 12 Sep 2027 date for unfair-terms rules on older contracts was not confirmed on any fetched page (unverified).

## L5-F30 — GPSR: every online offer to EU consumers must show manufacturer and safety data (since 13 Dec 2024)
- **What:** The General Product Safety Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2023/988) applies from 13 Dec 2024 in every Member State after an 18-month transition (IHK Bochum). For distance sales, each offer must show, before purchase, the manufacturer's name, trade name or trademark, postal and electronic address; product identification (image, type); the EU responsible person if the manufacturer is outside the EU (Art. 16(1)); and warnings or safety information (Art. 19). Online marketplaces (Art. 22) must register their single point of contact on the Safety Gate portal, act on authority orders within two working days, process product-safety notices within three working days, and inform consumers about recalls. Source: https://www.ihk.de/bochum/hauptnavigation/branchenbetreuung/handel/produktsicherheitsverordnung-neuerungen-ab-dezember-2024-6300630 (accessed 2026-10-29).
- **Who is affected / count:** Online sellers of non-food consumer products, including small shops on Allegro, Amazon, eBay, Etsy or their own store; a German checklist found in search says the rules apply regardless of company size (search summary). Count unverified.
- **What it forces or enables:** Bulk-filling GPSR fields (manufacturer, responsible person, warnings, product images) across hundreds of listings in shop platforms and marketplace feeds; checking supplier documents for missing data; extracting warnings from labels and manuals (see L5-F27/F28); recall notice workflows for small sellers. Commission guidelines (Notice C/2025/6233, dated 21 Nov 2025 per search summary) include a checklist for marketplaces.
- **Caveat:** Secondary source (German chamber of commerce); the regulation text on EUR-Lex and the Commission GPSR page could not be read (bot challenge / 404). The guidelines' date is from a search summary only.

## L5-F31 — e-Doręczenia: all CEIDG sole traders needed an electronic delivery address by 1 Oct 2026
- **What:** The gov.pl e-Doręczenia schedule lists: public entities from 1 Jan 2025 (art. 155 ust. 2–6 and 9 of ustawa o doręczeniach elektronicznych); firms in KRS from 1 Jan 2025 (new) and existing KRS firms (art. 151 ust. 1); new CEIDG registrations from 1 Apr 2025 (art. 152 ust. 1); CEIDG firms registered by 31 Dec 2024 when changing their entry after 30 Jun 2025 (art. 152 ust. 2); local governments for the public hybrid service from 31 Dec 2026; courts, bailiffs (komornicy), prosecution and police from 1 Oct 2029 (art. 155 ust. 7); and a final date of 1 Oct 2026. The gov.pl main page carries the headline "e-Doręczenia: 1 października 2026 r. ważny termin dla przedsiębiorców z CEIDG". Sources: https://www.gov.pl/web/e-doreczenia/harmonogram, https://www.gov.pl/web/e-doreczenia (accessed 2026-10-29).
- **Who is affected / count:** All Polish companies and sole traders, public bodies, and professions of public trust (adwokaci, radcy prawni, doradcy podatkowi, notariusze). Count unverified.
- **What it forces or enables:** Firms now receive official letters (tax office, ZUS, courts from 2029) in an e-Doręczenia inbox; search summaries say a letter counts as delivered 14 days after it arrives (fikcja doręczenia). Products: inbox monitoring and alerts for owners and accounting offices handling many clients, auto-classification and deadline extraction from official letters (using L5-F27/F28), forwarding into the firm's task tool.
- **Caveat:** The fetched schedule shows 1 Oct 2026 without naming the group; search summaries (secondary) say it covers CEIDG firms registered by 31 Dec 2024. The 14-day deemed-delivery rule is from a search summary only. Whether third-party tools can access e-Doręczenia inboxes via API was not checked (unverified).

## L5-F32 — Ustawa o ochronie sygnalistów: internal reporting channel for employers with 50+ workers (Dz.U. 2024 poz. 928)
- **What:** Ustawa z dnia 14 czerwca 2024 r. o ochronie sygnalistów, published in Dziennik Ustaw on 24 Jun 2024 as poz. 928 (dziennikustaw.gov.pl). It implements Directive (EU) 2019/1937. Readable fragments of the official PDF show art. 23 referring to the headcount on "1 stycznia lub 1 [lipca]" and "Do liczby 50", a 7-day deadline to confirm receipt of a report ("terminie 7 dni od dnia jego otrzymania"), and art. 58 providing a fine (kara grzywny). Source: https://dziennikustaw.gov.pl/DU/2024/928 and PDF http://dziennikustaw.gov.pl/D2024000092801.pdf (accessed 2026-10-29).
- **Who is affected / count:** Per search summaries: employers where at least 50 people do paid work as of 1 January or 1 July (counted per art. 23), entities in certain sectors regardless of headcount, and gminy/powiaty above 10,000 residents. Count unverified.
- **What it forces or enables:** A compliant, confidential reporting channel (web form, hotline, anonymous mailbox), a register of reports, 7-day acknowledgement and feedback deadline tracking, and a written procedure consulted with staff. A small SaaS can sell this to 50–250-person firms that do not want an enterprise GRC tool; voice intake (L5-F25) and triage (L5-F28) are add-ons.
- **Caveat:** The entry-into-force date of 25 Sep 2024 comes from search summaries only; the PDF's final article was not readable after local text extraction (Polish characters lost). The 3-month feedback deadline and the penalty start date (one source says 1 Jan 2025) are unverified. isap.sejm.gov.pl blocked the fetch with a bot check.

## L5-F33 — Prawo komunikacji elektronicznej: new cookie and single marketing-consent rules (in force since 10 Nov 2024)
- **What:** Ustawa z dnia 12 lipca 2024 r. – Prawo komunikacji elektronicznej, Dz.U. 2024 poz. 1221, published 9 Aug 2024 (dziennikustaw.gov.pl). ITwiz says it applies from 10 Nov 2024, replaces the old Prawo telekomunikacyjne, introduces one marketing consent (art. 398) that also covers business recipients ("abonentów lub użytkowników końcowych"), and requires shops to adjust cookies and data collection; sending commercial information without consent can bring a fine of up to 3% of annual revenue or 1,000,000 zł. Sources: https://dziennikustaw.gov.pl/DU/2024/1221, https://itwiz.pl/jak-nowe-prawo-komunikacji-elektronicznej-wplynie-na-branze-e-commerce/ (accessed 2026-10-29).
- **Who is affected / count:** Every website and app operator in Poland that stores or reads data on users' devices, and every firm doing email/SMS/phone marketing, including B2B. Count unverified.
- **What it forces or enables:** Cookie banner and consent logging tuned to art. 399–400 PKE, a consent register proving the single art. 398 consent, cleaning of old B2B contact lists (cold mailing to addresses from public registries is restricted per ITwiz), and audits that scan a site for trackers set before consent.
- **Caveat:** The cookie article (art. 399) wording comes from search summaries of a secondary legal database, not from the official text. Whether UKE or UODO enforces the cookie rule is unclear (one secondary summary says Prezes UKE). The 3% / 1,000,000 zł fine figures are from ITwiz, not the statute (unverified against the act).

## L5-F34 — ETS2: fuel suppliers report emissions now, surrender allowances from 2028
- **What:** The Commission's ETS2 page says regulated entities had to hold a greenhouse gas emissions permit by 1 Jan 2025; monitoring and reporting began in 2025; annual reports are due by 30 April; "From 2026, the data for a given year will have to be verified by an accredited verifier"; ETS2 "will become fully operational in 2028", when entities surrender allowances by 31 May of the following year. Regulated entities are "fuel suppliers, rather than end consumers". Source: https://climate.ec.europa.eu/eu-action/carbon-markets/ets2-buildings-road-transport-and-additional-sectors_en (accessed 2026-10-29).
- **Who is affected / count:** Fuel suppliers releasing fuel for buildings, road transport and additional sectors (directly); households and firms indirectly through fuel prices. Polish count unverified.
- **What it forces or enables:** Monitoring-plan and annual emissions report preparation for small fuel distributors (e.g. local heating-oil, LPG or coal sellers), data collection ready for verification, and later cost pass-through calculators for heating and fleet customers.
- **Caveat:** The page does not mention any 2027 start or a delay; reports of a postponement were not checked (unverified). A thin market for a solo developer; included because the obligation is concrete and dated.
