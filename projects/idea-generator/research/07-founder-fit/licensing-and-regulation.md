# Licensing and regulatory exposure: where the "won't do a financial or medical licence" line falls

Lens 7 findings on what a solo product can and cannot do without a licence. All sources accessed 2026-10-29. **None of this is legal advice**; each finding says which side of the founder's red line the activity sits on and what a lawyer would need to confirm. IDs `L7-Fnn` are stable anchors.

### L7-F09 — Holding or moving other people's money in Poland needs a KNF licence or registration; the lowest tier (MIP) is under proposal for abolition
- **Evidence:** law-firm article (RPMS, published 9 Sep 2026, updated 11 Sep 2026): 291 entities were entered in the register of małe instytucje płatnicze (MIP) since 2018; 47 applied for a krajowa instytucja płatnicza (KIP) licence and 10 obtained one; initial capital for a KIP is the equivalent of EUR 20,000 (money remittance only), EUR 50,000 (payment initiation) or EUR 125,000 (other payment services); in August 2026 UKNF proposed abolishing the MIP category (draft UC132, "not yet law"): https://rpms.pl/przejscie-z-mip-do-kip/ . Another summary (comparic.pl, secondary) says a MIP is limited to EUR 1,500,000 average monthly payment volume and KNF supervision of MIPs is intensive: https://comparic.pl/czym-jest-mala-instytucja-platnicza-i-kto-moze-ja-zalozyc/ .
- **Reading:** any idea that pools, splits, escrows, collects or forwards payments (deposit handling for salons, marketplace payouts, payment reminders that receive the money) is on the founder's red line ("financial licence"). The capital figures alone exceed the 500 PLN/month budget by orders of magnitude. The escape is F10.
- **Confidence:** medium (law-firm article; statute text not read).

### L7-F10 — Stripe Connect is available to platforms in Poland and moves onboarding/KYC and payouts to Stripe, which lets a software platform route money without being the payment institution (licence position of the platform is not stated by Stripe)
- **Evidence:** Stripe docs list PL among Connect countries; Connect "Onboard and verify sellers using connected accounts with Stripe-hosted flows" and runs payments and payouts; use cases include SaaS platforms where connected accounts "accept payments directly": https://docs.stripe.com/connect/overview . A Stripe support page (March 2026) says Stripe is updating KYC for connected accounts in Europe, which platforms must handle in their integration: https://support.stripe.com/questions/europe-updated-verification-requirements-for-connected-accounts-of-platforms . Stripe's cross-border payout guide notes that Stripe's licence is one option and mentions a money-transmitter licence "if you manage your customers' funds" (US wording): https://docs.stripe.com/docs/connect/cross-border-payouts (the last two as reported by search results, not read in full).
- **Reading:** the standard pattern is "connected accounts take the money, the platform takes an application fee". Whether this fully removes the platform's own PSD2 duties in Poland is **unverified**; needs a lawyer or a written answer from Stripe. A plain SaaS subscription billed to the founder's own Stripe account has none of this exposure.
- **Confidence:** medium for availability, low for the legal conclusion.

### L7-F11 — Account-information (open-banking) access is a registered payment service in Poland; requirements could not be verified
- **Evidence:** searches for AISP registration in Poland returned only generic KNF-register pages (one article lists duties after entry: follow KNF recommendations, notify changes, a supervision contribution based on user count × PLN 1 × a rate not above 15%): https://e-prawnik.pl/artykuly/fintechy-moga-bez-przeszkod-rejestrowac-dzialalnosc-w-polsce.html . Insurance, capital and application details **not verified** (the searches returned insurance-agent material instead).
- **Reading:** any product that reads a client's bank account directly (cash-flow dashboards, automatic invoice-payment matching) either needs the AISP registration or must use a licensed aggregator's API and contract. Treat as "licence-adjacent: avoid or buy". Bank-statement *file* upload (MT940/CSV) avoids the issue.
- **Confidence:** low.

### L7-F12 — EU AI Act Article 50 transparency duties applied from 2 Aug 2026 and were not delayed by the AI Omnibus; fines up to EUR 15 million or 3% of turnover
- **Evidence:** Goodwin alert (Aug 2026): chatbots must tell users they talk to AI unless obvious; synthetic audio/image/video/text must be machine-readable-marked; deployers must disclose deepfakes; AI Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force since 27 Jul 2026, delays high-risk (Annex III) to 2 Dec 2027 and Annex I to 2 Aug 2028 but not Article 50; generative systems already on the market have until 2 Dec 2026 for marking; Article 99(4) fines: https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force (accessed 2026-10-29; the OJ publication was not checked against EUR-Lex).
- **Reading:** an LLM-powered product with a chat interface or text generation inherits a small, cheap-to-meet duty (a disclosure line and output marking via provider features). This is "normal GDPR plus a label", not a licence. It is still a documented exposure for L7 = 5 claims: ideas that generate customer-facing text should budget the marking work. Whether plain-text outputs reviewed by a human fall under marking duties is **unverified**.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### L7-F13 — Administrative booking/scheduling software is generally outside EU medical-device rules, but the stated intended purpose decides
- **Evidence:** commentary on MDCG 2019-11 (rev. 1, June 2025) lists hospital administration, scheduling and billing software as generally not medical devices, while diagnosis or dose-calculation software is a device; the guidance is non-binding and intended purpose decides: https://openregulatory.com/mdcg/mdcg-2019-11 and the Commission's document https://ec.europa.eu/docsroom/documents/37581/attachments/1/translations/en/renditions/native (accessed 2026-10-29; commentary read via search summary, EC PDF not read).
- **Reading:** a booking tool for clinics, physios, dentists or vets can be sold without a medical licence if the product claims, copy and features stay administrative (no triage, no clinical values, no treatment suggestions). Adding "symptom checker" or clinical notes with AI summaries moves it toward the founder's red line.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### L7-F14 — Giving individual tax advice for a fee is a reserved activity in Poland; calculators and generic guides are a grey zone that was not resolved
- **Evidence:** the Ustawa o doradztwie podatkowym lists as tax advice "udzielanie podatnikom, płatnikom i inkasentom, na ich zlecenie lub na ich rzecz, porad, opinii i wyjaśnień z zakresu ich obowiązków podatkowych"; art. 3 ust. 1 reserves it to tax advisers, advocates, radcowie prawni and auditors (art. 4 exceptions for members-only organisations) – per a search-result summary of the act at https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2025/1882/text/T/D20251882L.pdf and https://iexpert.pl/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/ustawa-o-doradztwie-podatkowym-z-dnia-5-lipca-1996.pdf (accessed 2026-10-29; text not read directly). No ruling on software, calculators or "KSeF correction wizards" was found. A draft amendment (Sejm print 1729) widens item 1; adoption status **unverified**.
- **Reading:** products that tell a specific taxpayer "do X in your case" (KSeF correction wizard, tax-status checker, [L6 seed 9](../06-pain-evidence/README.md#seeds)) sit near this line; products that format, validate or transport documents do not. Mitigation: neutral wording, "informational, no advice" and a review by an accountant or lawyer before launch.
- **Confidence:** low-medium.

### L7-F28 — Products that process clients' invoices or customer lists make the founder a GDPR processor: a written Art. 28 contract and sub-processor control are mandatory, and LLM providers become sub-processors
- **Evidence:** GDPR Art. 28(3) requires a contract covering subject-matter, duration, nature and purpose, data types and data-subject categories; processing only on documented instructions; confidentiality of staff; Art. 32 security; deletion or return at the end; audit support; and Art. 28(2)/(4): prior written (specific or general) authorisation for sub-processors, same obligations imposed by contract, the processor "remains fully liable": https://gdpr-info.eu/art-28-gdpr/ (unofficial mirror of the regulation text, accessed 2026-10-29; UODO's own guidance page was not reachable for this question).
- **Reading:** this is "normal GDPR" in the L7 anchor, but concrete: a standard DPA template, a published sub-processor list and a deletion path must exist before the first B2B customer, especially accountants (they process their clients' data and will ask). If invoice content goes to an LLM via OpenRouter and then to a model vendor, each is a sub-processor in the chain; data-location and retention terms of those providers were **not checked** (unverified). Running a model locally or a vendor with EU hosting changes this; neither costed here.
- **Confidence:** high on the legal text, low on the LLM-chain specifics.
