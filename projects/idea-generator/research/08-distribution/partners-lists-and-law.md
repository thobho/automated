# Lens 8 — Partnerships, listable audiences and the rules for cold outreach

Part of [lens 8](README.md). All sources accessed 2026-10-29. Not legal advice; legal readings are from secondary sources unless stated, and a Polish lawyer should confirm before a campaign.

## L8-F08 — ifirma pays a flat bounty per referral, including for accounting offices
ifirma's partner programme pays PLN 100 per referred accounting office, PLN 50 per referred online-bookkeeping customer and PLN 10 per Faktura+ customer. The referred customer must register via the partner's link or code, sign the agreement and pay the invoice; no commission if the person was already an ifirma customer; payout above PLN 500 via the partner's own invoice (below that on request); invoices paid within 14 days. Source: https://www.ifirma.pl/program-partnerski (accessed 2026-10-29). Implication: this is how accounting-office referral is priced by an incumbent. A new tool for accounting offices that wants offices as a sales channel must offer comparable money or a clear time-saving, since the incumbents' flat bounties are small (PLN 10–100) and therefore do not lock offices in. Also a model for the founder's own programme: bounty per paying customer, paid after the customer pays.

## L8-F09 — wFirma has two partner tiers: a rev-share on add-ons and 30% for implementation partners
(a) Every wFirma account verified as an accounting office is automatically in a programme that pays commission on its clients' purchase of two add-ons (Magazyn, Rzetelny kontrahent), settled monthly; the percentage is not stated on the page. Source: https://pomoc.wfirma.pl/-program-partnerski-dla-biur-rachunkowych (accessed 2026-10-29). (b) The "partner wdrażający" programme: only entrepreneurs; benefits include affiliate link, training, partner-catalogue logo; commission 30% of the net price of base packages bought via the partner's link, monthly, only against the partner's VAT invoice, clawed back on cancellations; partners must actively acquire clients and are told not to push competing services; recommended implementation rate at least PLN 350 net per hour. Source: https://pomoc.wfirma.pl/-regulamin-programu-partner-wdrazajacy (accessed 2026-10-29). Implication: accounting offices already earn recurring commission from software vendors, so an offering with a built-in rev-share (e.g. 20–30% recurring) is the norm, not an extra. The wFirma terms also show that vendor programmes bind partners to exclusivity-like duties (§ 9(1)), so a tool that integrates with several accounting programmes is a neutral alternative.

## L8-F10 — CEIDG data warehouse: free, API, but login with Profil Zaufany; e-mail fields unconfirmed
dane.biznes.gov.pl ("Hurtownia danych CEIDG i Biznes.gov.pl") offers downloads, statistics and an API; an account is required, sign-in uses Profil Zaufany, and a data-protection statement must be accepted. Source: https://dane.biznes.gov.pl/ (accessed 2026-10-29). A Polish business-advice portal says registration and downloading are free: https://poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl/-hurtownia-danych-ceidg-do-czego-sluzy (via search summary, accessed 2026-10-29). Whether business e-mail addresses are in the dataset I could not confirm (unverified); the fetched pages do not list fields. Implication: selecting a segment (PKD 69.20.Z accounting, 96.02.Z hairdressing/beauty) is free and scriptable; contacting them is the legal problem (F13–F15).

## L8-F11 — REGON BIR API: free key by e-mail, generous limits
GUS's BIR1 service searches REGON data by REGON, NIP or KRS number. Production key via e-mail to regon_bir@stat.gov.pl for commercial and non-commercial users; "Usługa i dane udostępniane są bezpłatnie"; limits 6,000 calls/hour in working hours (120/min, 3/s), more at night. The page does not list the returned fields (e-mail not confirmed). Source: https://api.stat.gov.pl/Home/RegonApi (accessed 2026-10-29). Implication: good for enriching a list you already have (NIP to PKD, address, size class), not for finding contacts. Caveat: it is a lookup, not a bulk export.

## L8-F12 — KIDP has a public lookup of tax advisers; its member count is out of date in sources I found
kidp.pl has a form "Wyszukaj doradcę podatkowego" (surname, first name, entry number, region, keyword) and a "Lista doradców" link; whether results show contact data could not be verified. Source: https://kidp.pl/ (accessed 2026-10-29). The only count I found is 9,017 tax advisers as of January 2020 (KIDP to the press): https://ksiegowosc.infor.pl/wiadomosci/3601761,Liczba-doradcow-podatkowych-w-Polsce-przekroczyla-9000.html (accessed 2026-10-29) — stale, current figure unverified. Other bodies: Krajowa Izba Biur Rachunkowych "over 300 members" (secondary summary, unverified); SKwP has a database of accounting offices that offices can join to promote themselves (search summary only, unverified). Implication: the KIDP and SKwP universe is thousands, not tens of thousands, which fits a first-100 plan but caps outbound lists; membership bodies are partnership targets (webinars, newsletters) more than lists.

## L8-F13 — Poland: PKE art. 398 requires prior consent for commercial e-mail, and it covers companies
Art. 398 of the Prawo komunikacji elektronicznej bans using automated calling systems and telecommunications end devices to send commercial information, including direct marketing, unless the subscriber or end user gave prior consent; consent can be given by providing an electronic address for that purpose; breach is also an act of unfair competition. Source (statute text as shown): https://standardyprawa.pl/akt/532/art/63603 (accessed 2026-10-29). Commentary says protection covers all subscribers including legal persons, in contrast with the older e-services act whose art. 10 covers natural persons only; the 2024 text states explicitly that direct marketing is commercial information: https://www.ifirma.pl/blog/zgoda-marketingowa-jak-prawidlowo-uzyskac-zgode-na-wysylanie-ofert/ and https://api.sejm.gov.pl/sejm/term10/interpellations/attachment/ATTDM3HHH/i12146-o1.pdf (search summaries; accessed 2026-10-29). Another article (4 July 2025) concludes you cannot e-mail an offer without prior agreement: https://poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl/-cold-mailing-a-zasady-przetwarzania-danych-osobowych (accessed 2026-10-29). Implication: classic B2B cold e-mail in Poland carries a consent requirement on the plain reading; whether info@/biuro@ addresses of companies are treated differently in practice is disputed (F15). Safer routes: inbound, communities, partner referrals, phone/LinkedIn after taking legal advice (the rule is worded around automated systems and end devices; its reach to manually written LinkedIn messages is unverified).

## L8-F14 — Germany: UWG § 7 requires prior express consent for e-mail advertising, narrow existing-customer exception
§ 7 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UWG: advertising with an automatic calling machine, fax or electronic mail is an unreasonable nuisance without the addressee's prior express consent ("vorherige ausdrückliche Einwilligung"). Abs. 3 allows e-mail to existing customers only if the address was obtained in connection with a sale, is used for similar own products, the customer has not objected, and a clear right to object was given at collection and each use. Source: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__7.html (accessed 2026-10-29). Phone advertising to businesses is allowed with at least presumed consent (mutmassliche Einwilligung), which is not available for e-mail: IHK Cologne and IHK Munich pages via search summary (https://www.ihk.de/koeln/hauptnavigation/recht-steuern/werbung-per-telefon-fax-und-e-mail-5224338 and https://www.ihk-muenchen.de/email-marketing/, accessed 2026-10-29). Implication: cold e-mail to German Steuerberater or craft firms is the riskiest channel in this lens; German reach should come from DATEV Marktplatz (F06), content in German and communities, not mail lists.

## L8-F15 — GDPR on top: legitimate interest for cold e-mail is contested, art. 14 notice still applies
Secondary sources disagree on whether generic company addresses can rely on legitimate interest (art. 6(1)(f)); one reports UODO finding a firm in breach for e-mailing addresses taken from a public forum; a named work address is personal data per a UODO position reported by KPMG (about former employees). None of the pages I could read quotes UODO guidance on B2B cold e-mail directly. Sources: https://poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl/-cold-mailing-a-zasady-przetwarzania-danych-osobowych (read), https://kpmg.com/pl/pl/wiedza/prawo/korzystanie-z-adresu-e-mail-bylego-pracownika.html and https://woodpecker.co/blog/pl/rodo-a-cold-email-faq-2/ (search summaries only; Woodpecker sells cold-mail software, so treat as interested), accessed 2026-10-29. Wolters Kluwer's article on marketing and GDPR (https://www.wolterskluwer.com/pl-pl/expert-insights/jak-prowadzic-dzialania-marketingowe-zgodnie-z-rodo) returned HTTP 403 to my fetch, so its claim about art. 398 and cold calling is unverified. Implication: treat outbound by e-mail as a legal-risk item for the validation plan (T-lens 9+); the cheapest compliant "outbound" is a public form or a request-for-consent step before any offer, plus publishing a free tool that people opt into.
