# Lens 8 — Search gaps, communities, ad costs and first-customer stories

Part of [lens 8](README.md). All sources accessed 2026-10-29. Search-demand evidence here is weak by design (no keyword-volume tool was available); autocomplete shows that a phrase is searched, not how often.

## L8-F16 — Polish KSeF searches show "free / no login / viewer / download" intent
Google autocomplete (suggestqueries.google.com, hl=pl, queried 2026-10-29 from my machine) returns, for the stem "ksef podgląd faktur": "podgląd faktury bez logowania", "podgląd faktury anonimowy", "podgląd faktury xml", "podgląd faktury pdf", "numer ksef podgląd faktury"; for "ksef program do": "…faktur darmowy", "…za darmo", "…pobierania faktur"; for "ksef jak pobrać faktury": "…zakupowe", "…wszystkie", "…starsze", "…do rachmistrza"; for "jak wystawić fakturę korygującą ksef": 10 suggestions including "krok po kroku", "na minus", "2.0", "fakturownia". Stems with no or one suggestion: "ksef import faktur do excel" (1), "ksef powiadomienia o nowych fakturach" (2), "baselinker faktury ksef" (1), "allegro faktury automatycznie" (0). Method: https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&hl=pl&q=<query> (accessed 2026-10-29). Implication: the long tail around receiving, previewing and downloading invoices is searched in Polish; a free browser tool targeting those phrases is a plausible organic entry. The empty stems (Excel import, notifications) mean low or no searched demand today, so those would need push channels, not SEO.

## L8-F17 — The free official KSeF tools are the SEO competition, and they need a login
Per a vendor article last updated 14 October 2026: Aplikacja Podatnika KSeF (ap.ksef.mf.gov.pl, free, login via login.gov.pl or qualified certificate; issue, upload XML, purchase invoices, PDF, UPO, permissions, tokens), the mobile KSeF app (login via the national node + PIN/biometrics), and e-mikrofirma for sole traders. Source: https://norman.finance/pl/blog/jak-wystawic-fakture-w-ksef-za-darmo (author sells accounting software; accessed 2026-10-29). A search for a no-login XML-to-PDF tool found no dedicated converter in the results; options found were the QR-code route for printed invoices, the MF app, and invoicing software that previews only invoices stored in it (e.g. Fakturowo: https://www.fakturowo.pl/pomoc/podglad-faktury-w-ksef.pdf, accessed 2026-10-29, free tier up to 10 documents/month after trial per the same summary). Implication: the "no-login" angle is a gap today, but the state tools improve and the privacy question (uploading invoices to a third party) is real; unverified whether a converter already exists below the first page.

## L8-F18 — German E-Rechnung search is crowded with free state validators and viewers
Autocomplete (hl=de, 2026-10-29): "e-rechnung validator" suggests "…baden-württemberg", "…bayern", "…nrw", "…elster", "…kostenlos"; "xrechnung viewer" suggests "…kostenlos", "…download", "…open source", "…datev"; "zugferd prüfen online" suggests "…kostenlos"; "e-rechnung lesen" suggests "…pdf24", "…datev", "…elster". Same method as F16. Implication: demand exists, but free viewer/validator tools (several federal-state validators, PDF24, open-source viewers) are already in the suggestions, so a plain validator is a poor SEO bet. Differentiation must be a workflow (DATEV hand-off, batch, archive), consistent with [L7-F21](../07-founder-fit/build-speed-and-libraries.md) that libraries make rivals cheap.

## L8-F19 — Wykop #ksef is an active developer-and-user thread, including tool self-promotion
The Wykop tag page for #ksef (mostly 2026 posts) shows users sharing tools (a browser-based, local-processing PDF toolkit; "KsefInvoice", a free issuing app for Windows and Linux), asking for alerts for new invoices ("KSeF has no native alerts"), complaining about mObywatel login and invoice corrections, and linking articles on 2027 fines and counterparty VAT risk. Source: https://wykop.pl/tag/ksef (accessed 2026-10-29). Follower count and traffic were not shown (unverified). Implication: a place to post a free tool and see real pain statements; the audience mixes entrepreneurs and developers, not mostly accountants.

## L8-F20 — Accountants' hangouts named in one commercial guide: Wykop, Elektroda, Złote Myśli, Facebook groups
A 2026 guide by an invoicing vendor says that on forums such as Wykop, Elektroda, Złote Myśli and Facebook accountants' groups, entrepreneurs, accountants and office owners compare solutions and repeat three main complaints (server overload at peak, integration problems). Source: https://kluczesoft.pl/wiedza/poradniki/ksef-opinie-forum-2026 (via search summary; commercial source, low reliability, accessed 2026-10-29). I found no group names or member counts in any source (unverified); GOFIN (forum.gofin.pl) returned HTTP 403 to my fetch. PARP's service database lists many KSeF training offers for accountants (https://uslugirozwojowe.parp.gov.pl/, search summary), which also shows how crowded the "educate accountants about KSeF" angle is. Implication: communities exist but sizes are unmeasured; the validation step must count members and posting rules directly in the groups (a Gap).

## L8-F21 — Google Ads: no verified Polish CPC; global B2B figures are USD 5–12 and Poland is claimed to be far cheaper
A 2026 Polish guide (updated 11 February 2026) gives global figures only: search generally USD 1–4, finance/legal/insurance USD 6–60+: https://landingi.com/pl/reklama-cyfrowa/koszty-reklamy/ (accessed 2026-10-29). A European guide claims Poland EUR 0.17 average CPC (EUR 0.40 e-commerce, EUR 2.80 legal) and Germany EUR 0.91 but cites a second site and gives no method, with inconsistent sample sizes (400 vs 420): https://codedesign.org/google-ads-cost-europe-your-complete-guide/ (accessed 2026-10-29); treat as unverified. A June 2026 agency analysis (USD 700k+ LinkedIn spend, B2B SaaS clients, May 2025–May 2026) found Google non-branded search USD 12.48 per click and branded USD 1.71 (global, no regional split): https://www.seo-day.de/news/article/linkedin-ads-cpc-benchmarks-how-costs-compare-with-google?lang=en (accessed 2026-10-29). Estimate (assumption, not a source): at a PLN 2–5 click price, PLN 500/month buys about 100–250 clicks, i.e. a handful of trials per month at a 2–3% click-to-trial rate (assumed); at USD 12 per click it buys about 10. Implication: ads can test a message in week 2 but cannot be the first-100 channel unless the Polish keyword is genuinely cheap; check Google Keyword Planner for the exact keywords (free with an account) before any campaign.

## L8-F22 — LinkedIn Ads cost USD 5–14 per click in B2B benchmarks; Message Ads cost per send is low
Same agency analysis: LinkedIn blended USD 11.12 per click; prospecting USD 13.94; website visits USD 6.75; lead-gen objective USD 31.29 (https://www.seo-day.de/news/article/linkedin-ads-cpc-benchmarks-how-costs-compare-with-google?lang=en, accessed 2026-10-29). A Polish guide lists LinkedIn Message Ads at USD 2–3 CPC-equivalent with USD 0.26–0.50 per send (landingi link above; vendor-style blog). The minimum daily budget was not confirmed against LinkedIn's documentation (LinkedIn help URL returned 404). Implication: at PLN 500/month LinkedIn yields roughly 10–40 clicks (estimate), too few to learn from except for a very narrow, high-value audience (tax advisers, e-commerce managers).

## L8-F23 — FeedbackPanda: a niche profession, a tool for a repeated chore, and word of mouth
FeedbackPanda (feedback templates for online English teachers) was built by Arvid Kahl and Danielle Simpson in 2017, reached about USD 20,000 MRR within nine months and about USD 55,000 MRR and roughly 5,000 customers in 2019, and was sold to SureSwift Capital in June 2019 for "seven figures". Simpson: "From the beginning, our subscriptions came in reliably through word of mouth." Source: https://theygotacquired.com/?p=712 (accessed 2026-10-29). Another article says the launch went through Facebook groups for online English teachers (https://draper.chat/blog/solo-founders-first-100-customers-2026, a start-a-business vendor's blog, figures self-reported); the first source does not mention Facebook groups, so that channel detail is unverified. Implication: the pattern is a co-founder who is a member of the target profession; the Polish equivalent is a founder with a spouse, parent or friend who runs a bookkeeping office or salon. Check the founder's network (lens 7) before choosing the niche.

## L8-F24 — Founder stories show distribution came first, product second
The same Draper article (vendor blog, self-reported, accessed 2026-10-29) describes six solo founders: Pieter Levels (a public spreadsheet filled in by about 100 people in a day, then about 2,500 e-mail addresses), Tony Dinh (about 18 months building an X following of about 45,000 and a 3,000-subscriber newsletter before launching Xnapper), Marc Lou (about 2.5 years of audience building across about 20 mostly failed products; ShipFast USD 250,000 in five months with no ads, per the article), Danny Postma (sold a USD 19 ebook on Gumroad, then announced Headlime to buyers; 80 waitlist signups on day one), Josh Mohrer (LinkedIn build-in-public) and Kahl. Its own conclusion: founders with an existing audience reached customers far faster than those starting from zero. Implication: in a 1-month window the founder has no audience, so channels that do not need one (partners, directories, searchable tools, a narrow list) matter more than build-in-public. I found no Polish or German solo-founder first-100 story with sources (see Gaps).

## L8-F25 — "First 100 Shopify installs" guides are vendor content with no verifiable case
StoreCensus's blog lists three tactics (e-mail stores that run a competitor's app, found via StoreCensus or BuiltWith; genuine reviews on complementary apps; pitching "best apps" roundup authors with free access), caps sends at 50–75/day, and names no founder, install count or timeframe; the author sells the lead-finding tool. Source: https://blog.storecensus.com/how-to-get-your-first-100-installs-as-a-new-shopify-app-developer-3-uncommon-but-proven-tactics/ (accessed 2026-10-29). Other guides in the search results (appgrowify.com, discovermybusiness.co) were not read. A claim that a Product Hunt launch gives 50–200 installs a day appeared in a search summary only (unverified). Implication: "competitor's users via technology lookup" is the reusable idea (list built from public store tech data), but its legality for EU recipients is the F13–F15 problem, so a compliant version targets people who asked (waiting list, free tool).
