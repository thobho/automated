# Lens 9 — Churn benchmarks for small-customer SaaS

Part of [lens 9](README.md). All sources accessed 2026-10-29, read through a summarising fetch tool. Benchmarks come from US/global vendor networks, not Poland; I found **no Polish or DACH SMB churn study** (see Gaps). Metrics differ (logo vs revenue churn, monthly vs annual), so each finding states what is measured.

## L9-F09 — Recurly billing-network data (July 2026): SaaS median churn 3.22% (2.16% voluntary + 1.06% involuntary); churn falls from 4.29% at USD 10–25 ARPC to 2.87% at USD 100–250
Recurly's churn benchmark page lists medians by industry (SaaS 3.22%, business and professional services 3.44%, travel/hospitality 3.91%, digital media 4.14%, e-commerce 4.25%, education 4.99%) and by monthly average revenue per customer (USD 10–25: 4.29%; 25–50: 3.84%; 50–100: 3.15%; 100–250: 2.87%; over 250: 3.07%); top-quartile SaaS 1.78% or below. The page calls the industry figures "annual" but the ARPC table "monthly"; it also contains conflicting SaaS figures (3.04% in one passage, 3.22% in the chart) and does not state a sample size. Source: https://www.recurly.com/research/churn-rate-benchmarks (accessed 2026-10-29). A search snippet mentions a "1,200 sites" sample for an earlier report; unverified for this page.
- **Reading:** these values are low compared with the other studies (F10–F11) and are probably annual rates for established subscription sites with payment-card billing; they cannot be used as a monthly SMB planning number. What is robust is the direction: involuntary (failed-payment) churn is a third of the total (1.06 of 3.22) and is highest for the cheapest plans (1.30% vs 0.18% above USD 250). Card dunning matters more for 29–49 PLN plans.
- **Confidence:** low-medium (inconsistent page, unit unclear).

## L9-F10 — ProfitWell-based expert benchmark: for B2B sold to companies under 1,000 employees (generally under USD 1K/month), "good" monthly churn is 2.5–5% and "great" below 1.5%; month-one churn is often 5–50%
Lenny's Newsletter (Lenny Rachitsky), built on expert input and ProfitWell founder Patrick Campbell's anonymised data on about 13,000 SaaS companies: SMB plus mid-market good 2.5–5% monthly, great under 1.5%; enterprise good 1–2%, great under 0.5%. The article notes first-month churn is much higher (5–50%). Price-point breakdown is paywalled. Source: https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/monthly-churn-benchmarks (accessed 2026-10-29).
- **Reading:** the planning assumption for this lens' rubric (3–6% monthly for score 3 in [criteria.md](../../method/criteria.md)) is consistent with this source. At 3% monthly churn the average customer lifetime is about 33 months; at 5%, 20 months (arithmetic, 1/churn). To hold 100 customers at 3% churn you must add 3 per month.
- **Confidence:** medium (expert opinion + vendor data, "good/great" are judgements).

## L9-F11 — Sub-USD 25/month customers churn about 6% a month vs 2.2% above USD 500 (ChartMogul, 2022); Baremetrics open-startup median ACV USD 552 with 11.1% monthly revenue churn; Tunguz rule of thumb 3–7% monthly for SMB
ChartMogul (post of 23 Feb 2022, anonymised aggregated data for Nov 2021–Jan 2022, sample size not stated): median monthly customer churn 6.1% for ARPA under USD 25 and 2.2% for ARPA over USD 500; best companies aim under 2%. Source: https://chartmogul.com/blog/good-customer-churn-rate/ (accessed 2026-10-29). Point Nine's summary of older studies: Baremetrics "open startups" (median ACV USD 552) median monthly gross revenue churn 11.1% and user churn 5.4%; Groove survey of 712 small-business SaaS (2013) 3.2% monthly user churn; Pacific Crest (306 companies with revenue above USD 2.5M, median ACV USD 21K) 0.69% monthly customer churn; Tomasz Tunguz's table 3–7% monthly for SMB, ACV under USD 1K described as "much higher" than above. Source: https://writing.pointnine.com/p/saas-metrics-benchmarking-your-churn (accessed 2026-10-29; the article's tables are images, figures come from the summary text).
- **Reading:** products priced at 29–99 PLN per month (about USD 8–27, at an assumed 3.7 PLN per USD, not looked up) sit in the ARPA band with the worst benchmarks: 3–7% monthly is the realistic range, with early-stage samples worse. To reach 10,000 PLN MRR at 99 PLN you need about 101 live customers; at 5% monthly churn that means roughly 5 gross adds per month just to stand still. Higher price (299+ PLN) or B2B accounting-office customers move the product toward the 1–2% band.
- **Confidence:** medium (old data, mixed definitions, but consistent across 4 sources).

## L9-F12 — Annual billing cuts churn in every source, but only one is first-party: Buffer's monthly customers churned 7% a month vs 2.4% for annual
Baremetrics' article quotes Buffer's internal data (monthly plans 7% per month, annual plans 2.4% equivalent monthly; average lifetime 14 vs 40 months) and unsourced tables (monthly 8.5–12%, annual 3.1–7% a year; involuntary 7–14% vs 0.5–1% a year; monthly users 3x more likely to cancel in 90 days). It does not contain the ProfitWell "941 businesses" claim that a search summary attributed to it. Source: https://baremetrics.com/blog/annual-vs-monthly-pricing-better-retention (accessed 2026-10-29). Claims such as "annual billing churns 9% vs 5% (ProfitWell, 941 businesses)" or "Chargebee 2,000 businesses: +15–20 points retention" appeared only in vendor blogs in search results and are **unverified**.
- **Reading:** annual plans (or annual discounts) are the cheapest churn lever for a solo founder, they also remove the 1 zł fixed card fee 11 times a year (F01) and give cash up front, but they delay churn signals. KSeF/accounting products with a yearly rhythm (tax year, JPK) suit annual billing; salon or seller tools usually sell monthly (Fresha PL: PLN 29/month, [L9-F13](switching-and-lockin.md)).
- **Confidence:** medium for direction, low for magnitude.
