# Lens 9 — Switching costs, lock-in and moats

Part of [lens 9](README.md). All sources accessed 2026-10-29. Competitor help pages are marketing-influenced; each finding says so. Complaint evidence from lens 6 (L6-F08, F09) and price evidence from lens 2 (L2-F05) is referenced, not recounted.

## L9-F13 — Salon software: the moat is the consumer marketplace, not the calendar. Booksy takes ~25% of revenue from marketplace commissions; Fresha Poland charges PLN 29/month for the software and earns on payments and new-client fees
Booksy (Wikipedia, citing the company as of Sept 2026): about 350,000 business clients; about 70% of revenue from subscriptions, 25% from marketplace commissions, 5% from payment processing; 2024 revenue reported at USD 65.9M; merged with its largest Polish rival Versum in December 2020; reported total funding about USD 120M and a 20% workforce cut announced in September 2026. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booksy (accessed 2026-10-29; secondary). Fresha's Polish pricing page: Independent plan PLN 29/month, Team PLN 19 per member/month, a one-time fee on new marketplace clients (amount not stated on the page; a "Free" label conflicts), online payments 1.29% + PLN 0.49, BLIK 1.59% + PLN 1, automated texts 50 free then PLN 0.10 each. Source: https://www.fresha.com/pricing (accessed 2026-10-29). Booksy's Polish price and 45% first-visit commission: [L2-F05](../02-weak-competition/allegro-and-ecommerce-tools.md).
- **Reading:** two venture-funded incumbents give calendar software away or sell it at 29 PLN and monetise the consumer-demand side (commissions) and payments. A new calendar-only salon tool has no marketplace to offer and meets a 29 PLN anchor, so its pricing power is weak (estimate); its only structural advantage is no commission. A narrow salon feature (not the whole booking system) is a different bet.
- **Confidence:** medium.

## L9-F14 — Switching salon software is mostly a CSV job; history, reviews and payments usually stay behind; Booksy has no self-serve client export (competitor-reported)
Competitor migration pages say Fresha clients and appointments export as CSV; a competitor lists appointment history, marketplace ratings and reviews, transaction records and client notes as not transferable (SchedulingKit, marketing source). Bookedin's guide (page itself returned 403, content from search summary) says Booksy has no self-serve export: you ask support in chat and receive a file. Sources: https://schedulingkit.com/migrate-from/fresha (via search summary; not fetched), https://support.bookedin.com/hc/en-us/articles/4808208961300 (403 on fetch; search summary only), accessed 2026-10-29. Forced Versum → Booksy migration complaints: [L6-F09](../06-pain-evidence/ecommerce-and-local-services-pl.md) (9 of 300 reviews).
- **Reading:** a salon that wants to leave can take its client list (the asset it cares about), so lock-in is weak per se; the real friction is operational (retraining staff, rebuilding the online-booking link customers already use, losing marketplace-sourced clients). A migration importer ("bring your Booksy/Versum/Fresha CSV in 5 minutes") lowers acquisition friction but also shows that a rival can import yours.
- **Confidence:** low-medium (vendor and summary sources only).

## L9-F15 — Accounting software: wFirma imports contractors and products from listed programs or CSV, but "does not apply to invoices"; accounting-office clients stay for the history
wFirma help, "Import z innych programów": supported sources are IAI-Shop, Infakt, Insert, Sage Symfonia Faktura, Mega-Tech Faktura Small Business, Asseco Wapro WF-MAG, Uber or any system producing a CSV; data covered are kontrahenci and towary; invoices are explicitly not covered by the CSV route; import sits under USTAWIENIA » INNE » IMPORT I EKSPORT DANYCH; Infakt contractors can be pulled with an API key. iFirma is not on the list (I found no iFirma export documentation; unverified). Source: https://pomoc.wfirma.pl/-import-z-innych-programow (accessed 2026-10-29). Also the help centre has a separate article on moving full accounting (pełna księgowość) between systems mid-year (title only seen in search results: https://pomoc.wfirma.pl/-przeniesienie-pelnej-ksiegowosci-z-innego-systemu, not fetched).
- **Reading:** switching an invoicing/accounting tool means re-keying or manually migrating invoices and a year of tax records, which is a genuine switching cost (estimate: hours, not minutes, for an active JDG). This protects incumbents (wFirma, iFirma, Infakt) from small challengers and protects a challenger once it holds a year of a client's data. KSeF may weaken this, because issued/received invoices will sit in a central system and be re-downloadable by any tool (inferred from the KSeF design; not verified in this lens; see lens 5 for KSeF dates).
- **Confidence:** medium.

## L9-F16 — EU Data Act Art. 29: from 12 January 2027 providers of data processing services may not charge switching fees; whether small SaaS is in scope is unverified
Article 29 as reproduced by Prighter: "From 12 January 2027, providers of data processing services shall not impose any switching charges" (Art. 29(1)); reduced, cost-based charges allowed 11 January 2024 to 12 January 2027 (Art. 29(2)); Recital 88: switching charges abolished three years after entry into force. William Fry gives 11 January 2027 (one-day discrepancy). The Article 2 definition of "data processing service" (cloud-style services, plausibly including SaaS) was not on the page, so scope for a Polish micro-SaaS is **unverified**; a Digital Omnibus proposal to exempt custom-made non-IaaS services for older contracts was mentioned in a practitioner note and not checked. Sources: https://prighter.com/resources/laws/data-act/articles/article-29 (accessed 2026-10-29); https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/data-act-deep-dive-part-3-switching-interoperability (via search summary; not fetched, accessed 2026-10-29).
- **Reading:** the regulatory direction is toward easier exit from cloud services, so a product whose only defence is "we hold your data" gets less defensible in the EU; export features may become an obligation for B2B SaaS (check scope before relying on either reading). Defensibility has to come from workflow, integrations, content and distribution.
- **Confidence:** medium on the dates, low on SaaS scope.
