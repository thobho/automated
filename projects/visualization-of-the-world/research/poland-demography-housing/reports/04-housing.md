# 04 - Housing: new dwellings, houses vs flats, stock, Poland 2000-2025

Latest year: **2025** for completions, stock and permits (GUS BDL, calendar year or 31 December [S41][S44][S45]); construction starts only to **2019** [S46]. Place classes as in `../README.md` (34 big cities fixed at 2025, small cities, villages; gmina-level sums). Definitions of "house" and "flat" and every proxy are in the README. Class sums were checked against the BDL national value: zero difference in all 243 variable-year checks (`housing_national_crosscheck.csv`) [S41][S43][S44][S45].

## 1. How many dwellings, where (completions)
- Poland completed **87.8 thousand** dwellings in 2000, 162.7 thousand in 2003 (a one-year spike, see breaks), 160 thousand in 2009, 135.8 thousand in 2010, 178.3 thousand in 2017 and a record **238.5 thousand in 2022**, then 208.3 thousand in 2025 [S41].
- **Big cities** completed 40.7 thousand (2000), 64.4 thousand (2009), 78.2 thousand (2017), 85.6 thousand (2022) and 81.3 thousand (2025) (derived from [S41]). **Small cities**: 29.4, 46.9, 40.0, 62.5 and 52.6 thousand. **Villages**: 17.7, 48.7, 60.1, 90.4 and 74.4 thousand (derived from [S41]).
- In 2000 the big cities took 46% of all completions and villages 20%; in 2025 villages took 36%, big cities 39%, small cities 25% (derived, `housing_completions_by_class.csv`). Per 1000 residents the village rate rose from 1.2 in 2000 to 4.9 in 2025, big cities from 3.8 to 8.1 and small cities from 2.3 to 4.4 (derived from [S41][S1]); big cities still build most per head.

## 2. Houses vs flats
- **Direct split, 2009-2025** (dwellings in new single-family vs multi-family residential buildings) [S43]: nationally houses were 68.2 thousand and flats 85.6 thousand in 2009, 80.4 and 93.6 thousand in 2017, houses peaked at 119.5 thousand in 2022 (flats 115.3 thousand); houses outnumbered flats in 2010-2015 and in 2022, and in 2025 houses 97.9 thousand vs flats 107.8 thousand [S43]. The rest (non-residential buildings, additions, conversions) is small and shown as `other`.
- **By class in 2025** (share of the class's completions) [S43]: big cities 13.6% houses vs 84.9% flats (11.1 vs 69.0 thousand); small cities 38.7% vs 60.2% (20.4 vs 31.6 thousand); villages 89.3% vs 9.6% (66.4 vs 7.1 thousand). The pattern is stable: in 2009 the shares were 13% / 84% (big), 35% / 59% (small), 89% / 8% (villages) (derived).
- Over time, big-city flats rose from 54.0 thousand (2009) to 69.0 thousand (2025); village houses from 43.2 to 66.4 thousand, with a peak of 81.9 thousand in 2022 (derived from [S43]). Small cities are the mixed class and swing with the flat boom (flats 15.9 thousand in 2013, 36.9 thousand in 2022).
- **2000-2008, proxies only.** Individual construction (BDL "private") [S42] was 35.5 thousand in 2000, 52.4 thousand in 2002, 118.0 thousand in 2003, 64.9 thousand in 2004 and 72.0 thousand in 2009 nationally; its complement (developers, cooperatives, municipal, TBS, companies) was 52.2 thousand in 2000 and 88.0 thousand in 2009. In the villages the individual form was 88% of completions in 2000 and 95% in 2004; in big cities 16% and 25% (derived from [S42]).
- **How good is the proxy?** Where both exist (2009-2017) individual construction is 2-8% above the direct house count (2017: 82.4 vs 80.4 thousand; 2009: 72.0 vs 68.2 thousand), so it is a usable house proxy at national level [S42][S43]. The narrower "own use" series [S41] is 5-16% below the direct count (2017: 67.7 vs 80.4 thousand) because developers also build houses. By class the proxy is looser: in big cities individual construction was 10.3 thousand in 2017 against 8.8 thousand direct houses; in villages 51.4 vs 53.8 thousand (derived).
- A second cross-check on buildings: 72.2 thousand new one-dwelling buildings in 2017 [S47] fit between own use and the direct house dwelling count.

## 3. Floor area
- Average useful floor area of **all** dwellings completed fell in every class: big cities 76 m2 (2000), 81 (2004), 74 (2009), 64 (2017), 62 (2025); small cities 87, 106, 93, 92, 78; villages 125, 138, 140, 130, 120 (derived from [S41]).
- Houses are much larger than flats in every class (2025): houses 126 m2 in big cities, 121 in small cities, 128 in villages; flats 52, 50 and 50 m2 (derived from [S43]). Houses shrank from 161 / 155 / 149 m2 in 2009. The decline of the all-dwelling average in cities is therefore mostly a flat-heavy mix plus smaller flats; in villages it follows smaller houses.

## 4. Housing stock
- Stock (31 Dec) grew nationally from 11.84 million dwellings (2000) to 16.16 million (2025) [S44]. By class: big cities 3.78 to 5.55 million, small cities 4.18 to 5.43 million, villages 3.89 to 5.18 million (derived from [S44]).
- Dwellings per 1000 residents: big cities 356 to 550, small cities 320 to 450, villages 267 to 341; persons per dwelling: 2.81 to 1.82 (big), 3.12 to 2.22 (small), 3.75 to 2.93 (villages) (derived from [S44][S1]). Average useful floor area of the stock: big cities 53.6 to 60.2 m2, small cities 58.6 to 69.8, villages 72.2 to 98.4 (derived).
- Census check: the 2021 census counted 15.23 million dwellings, of which 13.44 million inhabited [S48]; the BDL stock for 31 December 2021 is 15.36 million [S44] (0.9% higher, different reference dates).

## 5. Permits and starts
- **Permits** (dwellings in permits and registrations with a construction project, by year of permit) [S45]: nationally 178.8 thousand (2009), 138.8 thousand (2013), 250.9 thousand (2017), a peak of **341.2 thousand in 2021** and 266.4 thousand in 2025. Classes exist only for 2018-2025. In 2025: big cities 90.3 thousand dwellings, of which 6.4 thousand in single-family buildings and 81.6 thousand in two-or-more-dwelling buildings; small cities 67.9 thousand (15.4 and 50.9); villages 108.2 thousand (76.0 and 30.6) (derived from [S45]). Village permits stay house-heavy (70%) while big-city permits are about 90% multi-family and small cities 75%.
- **Starts** exist only for powiats, so only big cities vs the rest of Poland can be shown, and only to 2019 [S46]: starts in the 34 big cities rose from 40.0 thousand (2005) to 58.4 thousand (2010), 92.2 thousand (2017) and 95.3 thousand (2019), i.e. from 38% to 40% of the national 105.8 and 237.3 thousand (derived from [S46]). Small cities and villages are not separable and were not estimated.

## 6. Where GUS changed method, and other breaks
- **2002 and 2020/2021 stock rebasing.** The stock grows by the completions less demolitions except in 2002 (+492 thousand against 97.6 thousand completed), 2020 (+378 thousand vs 220.8 thousand completed) and 2021 (+169 thousand vs 234.7 thousand) [S44][S41]. These match the 2002 census and the recalculation on the 2021 census (the same pattern as population in task 01) and are statistical breaks, not construction. The exact GUS note was not found.
- **Forms of construction.** Until 2017 BDL's "private" means individual construction including private dwellings for sale [S42]. From 2005 table P3824 splits it into private for own use and "for sale or rent" including private ones [S41]; both versions exist for 2005-2017 and are kept apart. Pre-2005 own use is missing in BDL (flagged x) and left empty.
- **2018 definitions.** BDL tables are headed "definitions valid to 2017" / "from 2018" [S47]. A trade article says GUS changed its method slightly from January 2018 and moved individually built dwellings that are sold at once into the sale-or-rent category [S49]; I could not confirm it in a GUS note. In the data the own-use series moves smoothly from 67.7 thousand (2017) to 66.2 thousand (2018) [S41] and total completions have no visible break, so any effect is small at national level.
- **2000-2003 individual construction.** Individual completions are 35.5 / 40.6 / 52.4 thousand in 2000-2002 and jump to 118.0 thousand in 2003 and 64.9 thousand in 2004 [S42]. Village completions are 17.7 thousand in 2000 and 35.6 thousand in 2004 (derived). The 2003 spike and the low early years could reflect changes in registration or the building law; the cause was not verified, so class trends before 2004 should be read with care.
- **Territorial changes.** Class membership follows the urban/rural status in each year (README of tasks 01/02): new town rights move completions from villages to small cities without any construction change; not corrected.
- Permits are intentions dated by permit year and are not comparable to completions in the same year.
